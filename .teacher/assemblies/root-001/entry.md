## EXL3 quantization: transforms, LDLQ feedback, and packed trellises

### Problem

EXL3 introduces weight-only compression using Hessian-guided error feedback and 256-weight trellis codes, while avoiding a stored large codebook. Its documentation identifies it as a QTIP variant with different regularization and packing. [source; quantizer::call_b53cb232bc4c471ea4a9338823fa587a, quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901]

### Reasoning

The design separates expensive offline search from inexpensive decoding: quantization searches overlapping 16-bit codewords, but storage retains only K new bits per weight and decoding regenerates values procedurally. [source; quantizer::call_4f7c7e6dd8de4d598daf7d8d0746d573, quantizer::call_2aee39a68c8341fb8398e7ffc349ea44, quantizer::call_3e212332095641dbb2697882dc0a3b51]

### Impact

The quantizer produces packed trellis data plus FP16 input/output channel scales, consumed by LinearEXL3. These sources establish the representation and interfaces, not model-quality or throughput improvements. [source; quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901, quantizer::call_6b0fb89eab354da2a14852bfd5cfa2fb]

### Explanation

Entry and calibration interface. Linear.capture_H flattens activation batches into rows, accumulates XᵀX in FP32, and counts activation rows. A qmap group records its first layer so shared inputs are not repeatedly accumulated. Linear.convert_exl3 passes floating-point weights and H_data to quantize_exl3, preserves the bias, and replaces its inner implementation with LinearEXL3. The quantizer's weight convention is (input channels k, output channels n), transposed relative to the papers' W∈R^(output×input). This is the local connection to calibration and conversion. [source; quantizer::call_6b0fb89eab354da2a14852bfd5cfa2fb, quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901, quantizer::call_fd9a32020d384119ac589957636c537f]

Hessian preparation and factorization. finalize_capture_H divides by the sample count, adds sigma_reg·mean(diag(H)) to every diagonal entry, and defaults sigma_reg to 0.025—a 2.5% mean-diagonal damping term. It samples input signs, applies them to both axes, and applies normalized 128-channel Hadamards on both sides. block_ldl starts from Cholesky C, sets each D block to Cii Ciiᵀ, and right-multiplies block column i by Cii⁻¹ to obtain a unit-block-lower L satisfying H≈LDLᵀ. It explicitly restores identity diagonal blocks; finalize_capture_H then removes their scalar diagonal, leaving the strictly block-lower feedback factor. The transformed H and reusable factor/sign data are cached. [source; quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901]

Paper mapping: block_ldl and ldlq implement the block-factorization/error-feedback mechanism of QuIP# §4.1, with reverse traversal and H=LDLᵀ as in QTIP Algorithm 5, rather than literally following QuIP#'s displayed forward-traversal convention. For a current 16-input-channel slab, ldlq quantizes W_current + L_later,currentᵀ(W_later−Q_later). Within a buffer, addmm_ computes the local correction; prod_cache carries corrections from completed buffers. Thus later rounding errors influence earlier choices instead of every tile being rounded independently. The buffered recurrence closely corresponds to pinned QTIP LDLQ's WXWX and prod_cache updates, adapted to EXL3's already-transposed weights and optional CPU result buffers. [source; quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901, quantizer::call_0034ea2b8c7240c7b649a9d011c5d9a9, quantizer::call_a4d617c8e2ad4ba593b46d967a200e37, quantizer::call_9fad16c8d7424fb6a84f7e6af191c009]

Block feedback and trellis dimension are different quantities. ldlq takes 16 input rows at a time and groups each 16-output-column slice into a 16×16 tile. This corresponds to QTIP Algorithm 5 with Tx=Ty=16: feedback block size 16, but a 256-dimensional quantization sequence. D is computed but not passed into tile search; the trellis minimizes additive squared reconstruction error on the compensated tile. It does not directly minimize the complete, generally non-additive Hessian objective over the matrix. [source; quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901, quantizer::call_4f7c7e6dd8de4d598daf7d8d0746d573, quantizer::call_e063e58b689643ec96c7f67f84465b34, quantizer::call_a4d617c8e2ad4ba593b46d967a200e37]

Transforms are an adaptation of QuIP# Algorithm 3 and QTIP's incoherence processing, not an exact full-matrix RHT implementation. quantize_exl3 first divides output channels by signed RMS scales sv, normalized to mean magnitude approximately one, then applies the right Hadamard. It next computes input-channel RMS values, combines them with the shared signs and −1/codebook_scale in su, divides by su, and applies the left Hadamard. Both Hadamards operate independently on 128-channel groups. The comments explain that concentrating overall magnitude in input rather than output scales reduces overflow risk during GEMM and the output transform. These are computed scales, not QuIP# Algorithm 5's gradient-based fine-tuning. [source; quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901, quantizer::call_9fad16c8d7424fb6a84f7e6af191c009, quantizer::call_14dac6f2fdcb4289af9d48fafc57db60]

The corresponding reconstruction reverses these operations: with block-normalized Hadamards Ak and An and the final diagonal scales Du,Dv, the working matrix is R=Ak Du⁻¹ W Dv⁻¹ An, and reconstruction is Wq=Du Ak Q An Dv. The global scale is incorporated by multiplying R and dividing su by the same factor. However, finalize_capture_H transforms H using input signs and Hadamards only, before the weight-dependent channel magnitudes are available. Therefore the returned normalized proxy error is a working-space diagnostic, not demonstrably the unchanged original activation-loss objective after all channel rescaling. [inference; quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901, quantizer::call_9fad16c8d7424fb6a84f7e6af191c009]

Hadamard implementation. preapply_had_l/r promote to FP32 for matrix multiplication and restore the input dtype. get_hadamard_dt supplies scaled matrices; get_hadamard supports stored constants, Sylvester doubling, and native Paley constructions. Those general constructors are not additional quantizer transform modes: quantize_exl3 fixes both dimensions at 128. For the runtime-side interface, had_r_128 selects hadf_r_128_kernel, which loads FP16, optionally multiplies FP16 prescales, performs a four-element butterfly followed by 32-lane FP32 shuffles, normalizes, and writes FP16. Its 0.088388347648 constant represents 1/√128. [source; quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901, quantizer::call_02d20a7f06174a52b458f66d2d404271, quantizer::call_1914382b0c30432abae53ba6184f3ba5, quantizer::call_376fb8e1ce204cd1bf1d97a0959b93f6]

Exact decoder correspondence. decode_3inst implements QTIP v3 Algorithm 2 and pinned bitshift.py::decode_3inst: x=(89226354·state+64248484) modulo 2³²; mask with 0x8fff8fff; XOR with 0x3b603b60; reinterpret the two halves as binary16 and add in binary16. The mask permits sign, mantissa, and two low exponent bits to vary; the remaining exponent bits keep the operands bounded. The duplicated magic half is 0.921875, the precise value behind the paper's rounded 0.922. decode_pcb, decode_pcb_f, and decode_pcb_f_diff all select this decoder; the float variants promote the half result rather than defining a higher-precision codebook. [source; quantizer::call_3e212332095641dbb2697882dc0a3b51, quantizer::call_b0ebf4c032b440929bca0fffd8e60e1d, quantizer::call_e063e58b689643ec96c7f67f84465b34]

Decoder statistics explain the scale constant. Exhaustive scalar enumeration of all 65,536 states, with explicit uint32 wrapping and binary16-rounded addition, produced mean 0.000210441649, RMS 1.243710924485, population standard deviation 1.243710906681, range [−3.95703125, 3.97265625], and 10,598 distinct numerical values. Rounding the second moment and final square root to FP32 gives 1.243710875511, agreeing with codebook_scale=1.24371088 to its printed precision. The scalar transcription of the pinned reference decoder gave zero mismatches. This supports RMS matching—not a unit-variance assumption—and agrees with science/codebook_eval.py's explicit RMS calculation. [execution; quantizer::call_c83814e78e194603a017743d94b2c4ec, quantizer::call_3e212332095641dbb2697882dc0a3b51, quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901, quantizer::call_c69d7cc3c0614733b865c25276c323b2]

Outlier constants are conservative heuristics, not exact decoder limits. The inactive g_scale_outliers_binsearch counts |R·scale|>3.9 and targets 0.00025, or 0.025%, using 20 step-halving iterations. Enumeration found eight decoder states beyond 3.9: 0.012207% of uniformly selected states. The threshold is about 3.136 decoder RMS units, below both absolute extrema. A hypothetical Gaussian with that RMS would instead put about 0.1714% beyond 3.9; meeting the 0.025% target would require about 3.662 standard deviations, or scale≈0.85624. These computations clarify the threshold's meaning but do not derive its optimality or model the distribution of states selected by trellis search. [execution; quantizer::call_c83814e78e194603a017743d94b2c4ec, quantizer::call_c8dcad9479524186a829771325c41ed6, quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901]

The active global-scale choice is g_scale_mse_gridsearch, not the outlier search. It quantizes sampled, tensor-core-permuted tiles at each candidate scale and minimizes mean((Q(scale·tile)/scale−tile)²). There are 12 coarse candidates over [0.25,1.075], then 16 candidates around the best coarse result. This optimizes sampled direct error before LDLQ feedback, not the final Hessian proxy. The intended width-three wrapped-diagonal sampler actually repeats each tile three times because its column expression uses i+width rather than i+w. Thus width does not provide three distinct neighboring samples. [source; quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901]

Search mechanism and paper mapping. quantize_tiles_kernel specializes K=1…8 with QTIP parameters L=16, V=1 and T=256. Its dynamic-programming boundary has 2^(16−K) overlap states. For each outgoing overlap e, it considers all 2^K codewords (candidate<<[16−K])|e, adds squared decoder error to the incoming overlap cost indexed by codeword>>K, and saves the best predecessor. Two FP32 cost buffers and uint16 predecessor history support backtracking. This is a reduced-overlap implementation of the recurrence in QTIP §2.3 and bitshift_codebook.update/viterbi, not a literal translation: the reference maintains full-state costs and converts input sequences to FP16, whereas EXL3 searches FP32 input tiles. [source; quantizer::call_4f7c7e6dd8de4d598daf7d8d0746d573, quantizer::call_fd9a32020d384119ac589957636c537f, quantizer::call_b0ebf4c032b440929bca0fffd8e60e1d]

Tail-biting follows the two-pass structure of QTIP Algorithm 4 and bitshift_codebook.quantize: solve a sequence rotated by 128, extract an overlap near the original boundary, then solve the original ordering with that overlap fixed at both ends. This avoids storing an initial-state prefix. Algorithm 4 is itself an approximation to the globally optimal circular path. EXL3 also has a concrete implementation discrepancy: forward writes the newest costs into temp_costs, but argmin_cost reads temp_costs_inc. A scalar ownership trace confirms that after 256 steps these contain iterations 255 and 254 respectively. Therefore even the first-pass selection should not be described as an exact implementation of the reference algorithm. [execution; quantizer::call_c8dcad9479524186a829771325c41ed6, quantizer::call_4f7c7e6dd8de4d598daf7d8d0746d573, quantizer::call_e063e58b689643ec96c7f67f84465b34, quantizer::call_b0ebf4c032b440929bca0fffd8e60e1d]

Packing preserves the overlap invariant. Adjacent 16-bit codewords share 16−K bits; pack_trellis_kernel stores only each codeword's low K bits, using sixteen spans of sixteen weights so every span occupies exactly K uint16 words. It swaps adjacent halfwords for subsequent uint32 access. unpack_trellis_kernel reconstructs circular 16-bit windows ending at each weight's K-bit contribution, using modulo tile indexing and funnel shifts. This implements QTIP's tail-biting bitstream principle, but differs from reference bitshift_codebook.pack_trellis, which begins with the full first state and removes the redundant suffix. It is not a claim of byte-compatible serialization. [source; quantizer::call_2aee39a68c8341fb8398e7ffc349ea44, quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901, quantizer::call_b0ebf4c032b440929bca0fffd8e60e1d]

Scalar layout checks. A transcription of packing, uint32 halfword ordering, and funnel-window unpacking recovered every codeword in 20 constructed circular tiles for each K=1…8, with zero mismatches. Each tile occupies 256K bits, or 32K bytes. Separately, tensor_core_perm was verified to be a permutation of all 256 positions and argsort its inverse. A direct comparison against a simply transposed pinned QTIP permutation did not match; the two layouts must not be identified as the same permutation. [execution; quantizer::call_ae10361240694866bf597ddb52c7681f, quantizer::call_2aee39a68c8341fb8398e7ffc349ea44, quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901, quantizer::call_0034ea2b8c7240c7b649a9d011c5d9a9]

Why indices stay in tensor-core order. ldlq permutes each tile before search, but applies the inverse permutation only to reconstructed values used by error feedback. Encoded indices remain in lane-major groups of eight. On the consumer side, exl3_gemm_kernel computes the trellis offset as (lane/2)·16+(lane%2)·8, equivalently lane·8; dq_dispatch decodes those eight positions directly into two FragB objects used by ptx_mma_m16n8k16. Keeping this order avoids an additional decoded-weight permutation at that interface. See the quantized-inference chapter for the surrounding GEMM access pattern and kernel selection. [source; quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901, quantizer::call_c7fad27e66b34774a304b5a821e9bfbf, quantizer::call_405db0126d5244e5b34634732077d927]

Storage and memory tradeoffs. The emitted tensors are trellis shaped (k/16,n/16,16K), plus FP16 vectors suh and svh; the active format does not use the available one-bit pack_signs helper or a separate global-scale tensor. Scale overhead is 16(k+n)/(kn) bits per weight—0.0078125 for a 4096² matrix. Quantization has a much larger temporary footprint: its fixed 128-tile cost/history allocation requires 128·(8+512)·2^(16−K) bytes, calculated as 2080 MiB at K=1, 1040 MiB at K=2, and 260 MiB at K=4. These are allocation sizes, not measured peak VRAM. [execution; quantizer::call_ae10361240694866bf597ddb52c7681f, quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901]

Numerical and shape assumptions remain important. Matrices exceeding 5×10⁸ weights move the working weights and collected results to CPU, trading transfers and synchronization for GPU storage; prod_cache and working L remain on the compute device. The 128-channel transforms require divisible dimensions. ldlq checks k%16, n%128, and n%buf_size_k, but its reverse row loop also assumes complete buf_size_k spans along k without explicitly checking that condition. Cholesky needs a usable positive-definite Hessian; there is no retry path here for failed factorization or an empty capture count. Small 1e−10 scale offsets and denominator floors guard divisions but are not general FP16 overflow/underflow guarantees. [source; quantizer::call_4acdd439a01742a9b8579bdbce37c901]

Supported alternatives and their limits. The active 3INST code avoids codebook lookups and learned entries. QuIP# E8P instead uses a compact structured eight-dimensional lattice codebook, while QTIP HYB uses a small tunable two-dimensional lookup table; these are meaningful alternative quality/cache/tunability tradeoffs, not EXL3 options exposed by quant_args. codebook.cuh contains an inactive 2MAD decoder, but its uint64 intermediate arithmetic and 0.008415 scaling differ from the pinned reference decode_2mad's explicit uint32 truncation and division by 147.800537109375. It is related in construction, not an exact decoder mapping, and the inspected inference dequantizers directly call 3INST. [source; quantizer::call_3e212332095641dbb2697882dc0a3b51, quantizer::call_b0ebf4c032b440929bca0fffd8e60e1d, quantizer::call_c7fad27e66b34774a304b5a821e9bfbf, quantizer::call_e063e58b689643ec96c7f67f84465b34, quantizer::call_4d337711eff040b58aa9646f13b25c98]

### Uncertainty and limits

No CUDA kernel, PyTorch quantization, model evaluation, or performance benchmark was executed. The computations are standard-library CPU scalar checks.

Decoder emulation explicitly preserves uint32 wrapping and binary16 round-to-nearest addition. Aggregate statistics use binary64 accumulation; the reported FP32-rounded RMS does not reproduce a GPU reduction's ordering.

The scalar packing checks use constructed valid circular paths, not paths emitted by quantize_tiles_kernel. They do not validate CUDA synchronization or the complete quantization pipeline.

quantize_tiles_kernel's first-pass argmin reads the preceding cost buffer. Consequently, exact equivalence to QTIP Algorithm 4—and its empirical approximation quality—is not established.

The inspected sources do not justify optimality of the outlier budget, scale-search bounds, regularization strength, or memory threshold. Block-local transforms and channel rescaling also prevent directly importing the papers' full-matrix guarantees.

## EXL3 loading, reconstruction and direct quantized GEMM

### Problem

EXL3 inference must turn packed trellis states into linear-layer products while preserving channel scales, Hadamard transforms and the quantizer’s tensor-core ordering. This implementation provides both direct quantized multiplication and reconstruction followed by dense multiplication. [source; quantized-inference::call_1e16a410475a42dead5424a8d77841a1, quantized-inference::call_ffb15fe60cf441e19e9c2ecbba79a9f4, quantized-inference::call_57a5c46a53b249c38bd373e2b9bc1821]

### Reasoning

The two execution paths expose a memory-versus-computation tradeoff: direct GEMM decodes compressed tiles into registers, whereas reconstruction allocates a full FP16 inner-weight matrix for cuBLAS. The default crossover at 32 flattened input rows is an implementation heuristic, not a demonstrated performance boundary. [inference; quantized-inference::call_1e16a410475a42dead5424a8d77841a1, quantized-inference::call_fb6ec8eba7ff48bdb332a4e05a6df917, quantized-inference::call_da2c89e6fad24f08bbbaa5f04ec4f478]

### Impact

Linear layers can load EXL3 tensors preferentially, retain an FP16 alternative, and override reconstruction through the forward parameters. Both EXL3 paths share the packed-state decoder, but their accumulation and intermediate rounding differ. [source; quantized-inference::call_d1ce61dc203e499d9692ad5c7e9414e6, quantized-inference::call_1e16a410475a42dead5424a8d77841a1, quantized-inference::call_4946713eee294bd7974f23d5c5a4c9fa, quantized-inference::call_2fd635db46594ee192abc9f3fc5294d8]

### Explanation

Loading establishes the representation contract. Linear.load tries EXL3 under the primary and alternate keys before trying dense weights. load_exl3 requires trellis plus an input-side su/suh alternative and an output-side sv/svh alternative, loads them onto the selected device, and includes optional bias. LinearEXL3 requires an int16, three-dimensional trellis; packed signs are int16, while unpacked scales and bias are FP16. It derives the bitrate b from trellis.shape[-1] // 16. Although loading still requests an optional .scale tensor, the constructor rejects a non-None scale. The current quantizer instead emits suh, svh and trellis, with scaling incorporated into the channel vectors. [source; quantized-inference::call_d1ce61dc203e499d9692ad5c7e9414e6, quantized-inference::call_1e16a410475a42dead5424a8d77841a1, quantized-inference::call_df8fb28b8d134d7bb21de020c91c2ed0]

The transform order explains how compressed inner weights become a normal linear layer. For inner matrix Q of shape (k,n), the unpacked-scale path implements the algebraic weight D(suh) Hk Q Hn D(svh), where Hk and Hn are blockwise normalized 128-point Hadamards. forward flattens the leading input dimensions, computes xh = (x multiplied by suh) Hk, multiplies xh by Q, applies the output Hadamard and then svh, restores the output shape and adds bias. A packed sv selects sign flips instead of general output scaling. This is an algebraic description; the implementation introduces FP16 rounding between stages. [inference; quantized-inference::call_1e16a410475a42dead5424a8d77841a1, quantized-inference::call_2612d7d72682438db41c567bd6f65485, quantized-inference::call_ffb15fe60cf441e19e9c2ecbba79a9f4]

LinearEXL3.forward defaults to reconstruction when the flattened row count m is at least 32; params['reconstruct'] overrides that choice. Despite the variable name torch_mode, this branch calls get_inner_weight_tensor, ext.reconstruct and ext.hgemm—not torch.matmul. It allocates a fresh zero-filled FP16 (k,n) matrix on each call, then applies the output Hadamard separately. The direct branch calls exl3_gemm on the packed trellis. With packed sv, that kernel also performs the output Hadamard and signs; with the currently emitted svh, Python invokes a separate Hadamard and multiplies by svh afterward. [source; quantized-inference::call_1e16a410475a42dead5424a8d77841a1, quantized-inference::call_df8fb28b8d134d7bb21de020c91c2ed0, quantized-inference::call_2fd635db46594ee192abc9f3fc5294d8]

Packing stores transitions rather than independent 16-bit indices. Each 16×16 tile contains 256 positions and occupies 16b int16 words, giving trellis shape (k/16,n/16,16b) for b=1…8. pack_trellis_kernel keeps each state's low b bits, writes them in stream order and swaps the 16-bit halves of each 32-bit storage word. Dequantization recovers a 16-bit trailing window ending at position (t+1)b; adjacent windows overlap by 16−b bits, and modulo addressing wraps within the tile. The +256b offset keeps the beginning-of-tile window arithmetic nonnegative while implementing that wrap. [source; quantized-inference::call_166d63c7be614d809427ceb0c5fedebb, quantized-inference::call_0c07ccdb4dde4b589c0a3e291cbeaac6, quantized-inference::call_ffb15fe60cf441e19e9c2ecbba79a9f4]

dq_dispatch converts eight consecutive states into two FragB register fragments. Bitrates 1 and 2 use dq8 with four-state shift reuse, bitrate 3 uses two-state reuse, and bitrate 4 has a dedicated aligned extractor. Bitrates 5, 6 and 8 use two dq4 calls; bitrate 7 uses paired dq2x2 calls. These specializations exploit the lane-aligned windows rather than repeatedly extracting every state independently. decode_3inst_2 then performs unsigned 32-bit affine mixing, mask/XOR construction of half values, and a half2 addition. This is procedural decoding, not a table lookup or ordinary integer-times-scale dequantization. The alternative 2MAD decoder exists but is not selected by this inference path. [source; quantized-inference::call_0c07ccdb4dde4b589c0a3e291cbeaac6, quantized-inference::call_45b4ff4daf3541c2a90c7fe83cd7df78, quantized-inference::call_6bbe069e223b44bbb29c1c3372856a40]

Tensor-core order is retained to make those eight decoded values immediately usable. tensor_core_perm assigns lane t the rows 2(t mod 4), that row plus 1, plus 8 and plus 9, at columns floor(t/4) and floor(t/4)+8. ldlq applies this permutation before quantization, undoes it only for reconstructed values, and leaves encoded states permuted. In GEMM, load_frags requests offset floor(t/2)×16+(t mod 2)×8 = 8t and decodes directly into the two B fragments consumed by m16n8k16 MMA instructions. Thus the direct path avoids a decoded-weight rearrangement through shared memory. The quantizer chapter covers how these encoded states are produced. [source; quantized-inference::call_ffb15fe60cf441e19e9c2ecbba79a9f4, quantized-inference::call_fb6ec8eba7ff48bdb332a4e05a6df917, quantized-inference::call_6bbe069e223b44bbb29c1c3372856a40]

Reconstruction pays the layout-conversion cost explicitly. reconstruct_kernel processes a 16×128 region using 256 threads: eight warps decode eight packed 16×16 tiles with the same dq_dispatch used by GEMM. Each warp shuffles values from the lane four positions ahead, interleaves half values into a shared row-major tile, and writes contiguous output vectors. get_inner_weight_tensor returns only this decoded Q. get_weight_tensor additionally applies the left/right Hadamards and channel factors, making full weight reconstruction a distinct operation from the reconstruction used during forward. [source; quantized-inference::call_4946713eee294bd7974f23d5c5a4c9fa, quantized-inference::call_1e16a410475a42dead5424a8d77841a1, quantized-inference::call_2cac34182a2e48348b4877ec99b08221]

A bounded CPU check verified all 256 permutation positions form a bijection, the argsort inverse restores row-major order, and the reconstruction shuffle/store mapping restores all 256 labeled positions. It also compared scalar emulations of packing and the selected dq_dispatch extraction paths against circular bit-string windows: 32,768 windows matched across 16 deterministic random tiles at each bitrate 1…8. The emulation explicitly masked 32-bit packing arithmetic and modeled the swapped half-word storage. These results validate the checked indexing identities, not CUDA execution or the floating-point decoder. [execution; quantized-inference::call_4cebfc52595947b8b6b29939b74b4476]

Direct GEMM pipelines compressed data rather than materializing Q. exl3_gemm_kernel asynchronously copies row-major A and packed B tiles into shared-memory stages, loads A with ldmatrix, decodes B into register fragments, and invokes FP16-input/FP32-accumulator m16n8k16 instructions. All but variant 1 use two fragment stages; the source explains that alternating explicit iterations helps interleave dequantization without spilling fragment arrays to local memory. Work is sliced across the k×n tile space. Partial k sums are reduced within a block, then ordered across blocks by per-output-tile locks. Cross-block intermediate sums are stored in FP16 C, so FP32 MMA accumulation does not imply an entirely FP32 reduction. [source; quantized-inference::call_fb6ec8eba7ff48bdb332a4e05a6df917, quantized-inference::call_2fd635db46594ee192abc9f3fc5294d8, quantized-inference::call_6bbe069e223b44bbb29c1c3372856a40]

The optional fused output transform runs only after the final reduction. It reloads FP16 C, combines a four-element transform with 32-lane XOR-shuffle stages, normalizes, applies packed signs and writes FP16 output. The separate had_r_128 uses the same 128-point structure, with FP16 input prescaling and floating-point transform arithmetic. Its 0.088388347648 normalization represents 1/√128; the scalar calculation returned 0.08838834764831843. Fusion here removes a separate transform launch, but does not eliminate the intermediate global-memory C write/read. [source; quantized-inference::call_2fd635db46594ee192abc9f3fc5294d8, quantized-inference::call_2612d7d72682438db41c567bd6f65485, quantized-inference::call_4cebfc52595947b8b6b29939b74b4476]

Six native variants are available, expressed as (tile m,k,n; shared stages; fragment stages): 1=(16,16,128;3;1), 2=(16,32,256;4;2), 3=(16,32,128;4;2), 4=(32,32,128;4;2), 5=(64,16,128;4;2), and 6=(16,16,512;4;2). launch rejects k or n not divisible by the selected tile dimensions; partial m tiles are supported. The public EXL3 forward also requires FP16 input and k divisible by 128 through had_r_128, while output transform/reconstruction paths require n in complete 128-channel groups. The native comments are not a complete validation contract: actual launch checks and transform indexing determine these constraints. [source; quantized-inference::call_57a5c46a53b249c38bd373e2b9bc1821, quantized-inference::call_a664fafe103341b685b362989843904a, quantized-inference::call_fb6ec8eba7ff48bdb332a4e05a6df917, quantized-inference::call_2612d7d72682438db41c567bd6f65485, quantized-inference::call_4946713eee294bd7974f23d5c5a4c9fa]

select_kernel is a fixed shape/device heuristic. OLD and Ampere categories use variant 4 above 16 rows, otherwise variant 3 for n<2048 or variant 2 when n is divisible by 256. Ada uses a 50-million-weight threshold for small-m selection and an 8-million-weight threshold for larger m. Hopper/Blackwell additionally select variant 6 for m≤16 when n≥65536 or k×n≥200 million, provided n is divisible by 512; above 32 rows they choose variant 5 for matrices of at least 8 million weights. These numbers classify matrix size, not quantization accuracy. A positive force_kernel_idx bypasses selection; otherwise the Python caller passes −1. An incompatible selected variant returns zero, which LinearEXL3.forward does not inspect or replace with reconstruction. [source; quantized-inference::call_57a5c46a53b249c38bd373e2b9bc1821, quantized-inference::call_1e16a410475a42dead5424a8d77841a1]

Launch sizing contains additional resource assumptions. Every variant requests 90 KiB dynamic shared memory, identified in the source as the compute-capability-8.6 maximum. The slice count is min(SM count, floor(number of k×n tiles/12)); the source labels 12 experimental. Scalar arithmetic confirmed that the declared shared arrays fit the budget for all six variants at the maximum bitrate, requiring 11,776 to 40,960 bytes. It also showed that k=n=128 with a 32×128 k×n tile yields zero slices. Thus valid transform dimensions alone do not guarantee a nonzero launch grid. [execution; quantized-inference::call_4cebfc52595947b8b6b29939b74b4476, quantized-inference::call_57a5c46a53b249c38bd373e2b9bc1821, quantized-inference::call_fb6ec8eba7ff48bdb332a4e05a6df917]

The dense alternative shares the same linear-layer interface without the EXL3 transforms. LinearFP16 transposes loaded (out,in) weights into contiguous (in,out) storage, converts float32 weights to FP16, and invokes hgemm before bias and output reshaping. hgemm implements row-major multiplication through cublasHgemm by reversing operand order and m/n arguments, with alpha=1 and beta=0. LinearFP16_torch is also exported and wraps nn.Linear, but the inspected loader chooses LinearFP16, not that torch alternative. Reconstruction therefore reuses the dense cuBLAS backend while retaining quantized storage between calls. [source; quantized-inference::call_1932d59907964723b84ed037dfe3f6c7, quantized-inference::call_da2c89e6fad24f08bbbaa5f04ec4f478, quantized-inference::call_41c2e2a053604343b62e97916e7992d6, quantized-inference::call_d1ce61dc203e499d9692ad5c7e9414e6, quantized-inference::call_1e16a410475a42dead5424a8d77841a1]

### Uncertainty and limits

No CUDA kernel, cuBLAS comparison, model inference or performance benchmark was executed. The successful run was a standard-library CPU check of integer packing, indexing and resource arithmetic; it did not test decoder floating-point values, GPU synchronization or numerical agreement between execution paths.

Packed-input-sign support is incomplete in the inspected Python path: LinearEXL3 accepts su, but forward passes only suh to had_r_128. get_weight_tensor also uses tensor truth tests for su and sv rather than explicit None checks. These paths were not exercised.

The wrappers do not fully validate their storage contracts. In particular, reconstruct uses cols // 8 without checking the output-width remainder; direct GEMM does not provide an automatic fallback after an incompatible launch selection; and its slice-count heuristic can produce a zero-sized launch grid for small matrices.

Kernel thresholds and architecture categories are inspected dispatch policy, not evidence of optimality or validated hardware support. FP16 intermediate storage also prevents assuming bitwise equivalence between direct GEMM, reconstructed GEMM and full-weight reconstruction.

## Calibration, allocation and resumable model conversion

### Problem

The change introduces a conversion workflow that turns an unquantized model into EXL3 artifacts using calibration activations, per-linear bit allocation and resumable module-by-module processing. [source; conversion::call_9d2b40cccc554bb6b665e280ba5ead68]

### Reasoning

The workflow separates calibration statistics, bitrate selection, quantization and artifact assembly. Allocation is a fixed size-based heuristic: the collected Hessians guide quantization, not a measured search over bitrate assignments. [source; conversion::call_cce9dff1e4174d77bb097f15f6d990a9, conversion::call_9d2b40cccc554bb6b665e280ba5ead68]

### Impact

Conversion produces intermediate per-module safetensors and activation checkpoints, then assembles model shards, configuration and accompanying non-weight files. [source; conversion::call_9d2b40cccc554bb6b665e280ba5ead68, conversion::call_8da4ef03d346401aab6deb5459623bf0]

### Explanation

prepare() requires a working directory and, for a new job, input/output directories and a bitrate. Defaults are 100 calibration rows, 2048 tokens per row, a separate head target of 6 bits, 8192-MiB shards and a 60-second minimum checkpoint interval. New jobs save args.json and initialize ckpt/job.json with next_module_idx=0 and surplus_bits=0. Resume reloads those arguments and the checkpoint; normal differing nonzero overrides of bitrate, head bitrate and calibration dimensions are rejected, while paths, shard size and checkpoint controls may change. main() constructs Config, Model and Tokenizer and uses cuda:0, except for modules advertising prefer_cpu. [source; conversion::call_9d2b40cccc554bb6b665e280ba5ead68]

get_default_calibration() mixes C4, code, multilingual, technical, Wikipedia, TinyStories-style text and random token IDs with weights 10:15:15:10:48:10:20. Text-source row counts are floored with a minimum of one; random data fills the remaining requested rows, also with a minimum of one. Article-based processors concatenate articles until a row is full, discard overflow rather than carrying it forward, and request BOS/EOS on alternating output rows. Line shuffling and random-token generation each seed their respective RNG with zero. Raw-text processors instead slice one tokenized stream. These paths do not validate that enough articles or tokens exist to fill the requested shape. [source; conversion::call_32c398d9528d4612a7881d7107a353a8]

A bounded standard-library reproduction gives default source counts of 7, 11, 11, 7, 37, 7 and 20 rows: 100 rows, or 204800 tokens if every row is full. Requesting one row instead produces seven because every source has a one-row minimum. This checks the distribution arithmetic, not the corpus contents or tokenizer output. [execution; conversion::call_2ee0325924484acfbb857b2ba18165b0]

main() processes one top-level module at a time. For modules with quantization maps, it first forwards all current calibration states through the unquantized module using flash_attn_nc and a shared capture dictionary, retaining the first five outputs as references. After conversion, it forwards the same input states through the converted module and stores outputs on CPU for the next module. Thus later modules are calibrated on states propagated through already-converted predecessors, rather than an independent full-precision pass through the entire model. Modules without quantization targets are still saved and propagated; the final module only computes outputs for the first five reference rows. [source; conversion::call_9d2b40cccc554bb6b665e280ba5ead68, conversion::call_0034c7aa543148d792892e8c288fad58]

Linear.forward() captures its input before applying the linear operation. capture_H() flattens all leading activation dimensions into samples, copies them to FP32 and accumulates H += XᵀX plus the sample count. H is therefore an uncentered activation Gram matrix, not a Hessian obtained by differentiating the language-model loss. A qmap identifies a shared input: first_key allows only the first associated linear to accumulate, avoiding duplicate contributions when projections share activations. Each matrix has shape in_features × in_features. [source; conversion::call_1e4b09cdc5d541ed80d4115afa932442, conversion::call_0034c7aa543148d792892e8c288fad58]

The quantizer interface consumes that capture dictionary. finalize_capture_H() divides H by the sample count, adds 0.025 times its mean diagonal to every diagonal entry by default, applies shared input sign flips and normalized 128-wide Hadamard transforms, and computes a 16-wide block LDL factorization. The 0.025 constant is relative diagonal regularization, not an absolute threshold. The finalized flag caches H, L and input signs for other linears sharing the qmap. The converter supplies each linear with seed=top-level module index and K=its allocated integer bitrate. Linear.convert_exl3() passes FP32 weights in input-by-output orientation, discards the original inner layer, and installs LinearEXL3 with the returned tensors while preserving the bias. [source; conversion::call_fcc2e7116b4b41bebe097aa4b6eb9abc, conversion::call_1e4b09cdc5d541ed80d4115afa932442, conversion::call_9d2b40cccc554bb6b665e280ba5ead68, conversion::call_807ec78285a14212bdeb994d7e0d5aa2]

Memory is managed by staging rather than loading the whole model for conversion. Captured H matrices move to system RAM with their original devices recorded; original linear weights and biases are also swapped to CPU and restored one linear at a time. Quantization restores the needed H and keeps a CPU copy of the cached LDL factor. Calibration states reside on CPU between modules, and each processed module is unloaded. This trades host memory and transfer work for lower simultaneous GPU residency; the source does not quantify the resulting memory savings or runtime. [inference; conversion::call_9d2b40cccc554bb6b665e280ba5ead68, conversion::call_807ec78285a14212bdeb994d7e0d5aa2, conversion::call_fcc2e7116b4b41bebe097aa4b6eb9abc]

allocate_transformer() budgets B=int(target_bpw × weight_count)+incoming_surplus+1 and derives a base bitrate from B/weight_count, with a minimum of one. It considers 24 combinations: six fixed Q/K/V/O increment patterns crossed with four gate/up/down patterns, clamping each resulting bitrate to eight. It sorts candidates by weighted bit cost and chooses one near the budget, returning unused bits for the next module. These patterns favor particular projections without consulting capture statistics or proxy errors. allocate_linear() instead floors its effective budget to one bitrate, with a minimum of one but no eight-bit cap. TransformerBlock and Linear route their configured qbits_key into these helpers, allowing separate decoder and head targets. [source; conversion::call_cce9dff1e4174d77bb097f15f6d990a9, conversion::call_a94c6f10a2e142a5a4f7fb8304e955b9, conversion::call_1e4b09cdc5d541ed80d4115afa932442, conversion::call_9d2b40cccc554bb6b665e280ba5ead68]

The allocator has a subtle boundary condition: bisect_right(options, (budget,)) places the one-element search tuple before any equal-cost (cost, pattern) tuple, so an exactly matching candidate is excluded when a lower candidate exists. The explicit +1 adds one bit of slack but does not eliminate every equality case. A scalar reproduction with seven 128-weight projections selected 2688 bits from a 2689-bit budget; with 127 incoming surplus bits it still selected 2688 despite an available exact 2816-bit candidate. A target of 0.5 bits selected the minimum 896-bit candidate against a 449-bit budget, yielding −447 surplus. These are arithmetic edge checks, not model results. [execution; conversion::call_2ee0325924484acfbb857b2ba18165b0]

The workflow reports three different quantities. proxy_err is the quantizer's normalized H-weighted error in transformed/scaled weight coordinates, with its denominator floored at 1e−8. rfn averages the relative Frobenius output error against up to five retained unquantized-module references, dividing by the fixed num_ref_states=5. Reported final module bpw instead counts actual tensor payload bytes ×8 divided by module weight count. Consequently allocated K is not the complete storage bitrate: exported scales, biases and other module tensors contribute to the latter. Neither diagnostic is an end-to-end perplexity measurement, and printed timers are instrumentation rather than results observed here. [source; conversion::call_fcc2e7116b4b41bebe097aa4b6eb9abc, conversion::call_9d2b40cccc554bb6b665e280ba5ead68, conversion::call_8da4ef03d346401aab6deb5459623bf0]

After each module, conversion collects get_tensors() recursively and writes qtensors/<module-key>.safetensors. The active quantizer exports packed trellis indices and FP16 channel-scale vectors suh/svh; LinearEXL3 receives those tensors plus the original bias. Saving untouched module tensors through the same interface keeps quantized and nonquantized parts together. There is a path-handling hazard: main() passes an already work_dir-prefixed filename to save_tensor(), which joins work_dir again. An absolute work_dir avoids the duplicate prefix; a relative work_dir can produce a nested, unintended destination. [source; conversion::call_9d2b40cccc554bb6b665e280ba5ead68, conversion::call_fcc2e7116b4b41bebe097aa4b6eb9abc, conversion::call_1e4b09cdc5d541ed80d4115afa932442]

Checkpoint state contains the next module index, carried surplus bits and the list of propagated activation tensors—not partially accumulated Hessians or an in-progress linear quantization. At an eligible module boundary, the converter writes ckpt_new/job.json and state.safetensors, removes the previous ckpt_old, then renames ckpt to ckpt_old and ckpt_new to ckpt. Resume skips modules before the saved index and reloads the saved activations; work after the last checkpoint is replayed. There is no forced final checkpoint. The two renames are not one transaction, and prepare() does not fall back to ckpt_old if interrupted between them, so this is resumability machinery rather than a demonstrated crash-safe protocol. [source; conversion::call_9d2b40cccc554bb6b665e280ba5ead68]

compile_model() means artifact assembly, not native-code compilation. It reads qtensors through SafetensorsCollection and groups complete top-level modules into shards using tensor payload sizes. shard_size is multiplied by 1024², so its advertised MB unit is effectively MiB. A module larger than the limit is kept whole with a warning: the limit is a packing target, not a hard maximum. Output is model.safetensors for one shard or numbered model-xxxxx-of-yyyyy.safetensors files plus an index containing total_size and weight_map for multiple shards. [source; conversion::call_8da4ef03d346401aab6deb5459623bf0]

Assembly copies top-level auxiliary files while excluding original safetensors, their index, config.json and common weight/checkpoint extensions. It then rewrites the original configuration with quant_method='exl3', package version, requested decoder bits and requested calibration dimensions. The separate head bitrate is commented out of this metadata; the configuration is therefore not a complete record of the per-linear allocation. Existing output directories are only warned about, not cleaned, and an index is written only for multi-shard output, so reusing a directory can leave stale artifacts. [source; conversion::call_8da4ef03d346401aab6deb5459623bf0]

### Source notes

The executable entrypoint delegates argument handling and conversion to prepare() and main(); quantization itself is reached through Linear.convert_exl3() and quantize_exl3(). [source; conversion::call_18fd7337a065427d8047e24a64992ba2, conversion::call_1e4b09cdc5d541ed80d4115afa932442]

## Architecture dispatch and checkpoint-to-module mapping

### Problem

The model layer must translate architecture-specific configuration and checkpoint names into shared attention, MLP, normalization and output modules. [source; models::call_4e573b2a1bff4d1596a849083842f3fb, models::call_2bf97cffb54c404db531d664bc197d62, models::call_9d20ee4c565847cabb6c03320692f7de, models::call_4887351d9a60417a885bfddf6d6a5753]

### Reasoning

The implementation shares Llama construction with Mistral and Qwen2, while explicitly adapting Phi3's fused checkpoint projections and Gemma2's normalization, scaling and softcapping. [source; models::call_1d4523c7820144c2b2d2ca2288906842, models::call_547713695a844166803065afbd7898b5, models::call_9d20ee4c565847cabb6c03320692f7de, models::call_4887351d9a60417a885bfddf6d6a5753]

### Impact

Five architecture names are registered, but registration and matching tensor dimensions do not establish full reference-model equivalence: sliding-window configuration has concrete mapping gaps. [inference; models::call_4e573b2a1bff4d1596a849083842f3fb, models::call_01763895acc1474281f6e06d62047559, models::call_1e2940c5974e456483b2f31163df6f8b, models::call_d20039afd5964f91bbe3229fbc2f6f7c, models::call_2bf97cffb54c404db531d664bc197d62, models::call_9d20ee4c565847cabb6c03320692f7de, models::call_4887351d9a60417a885bfddf6d6a5753]

### Explanation

Dispatch uses config.json's single architectures entry, not model_type or remote auto_map code. Config.from_directory rejects missing, multiple or unknown architecture declarations and selects one of LlamaConfig, MistralConfig, Qwen2Config, Phi3Config or Gemma2Config. Model.from_config then instantiates config.model_class. A subtle distinction is that MistralConfig and Qwen2Config inherit LlamaConfig's assignment of LlamaModel: their registered model subclasses are thin wrappers, but the normal factory actually constructs LlamaModel for both. [source; models::call_4e573b2a1bff4d1596a849083842f3fb, models::call_3ac63e7f2f9b47a19663bc5481cec4f4, models::call_c095ad5e75eb4035965bed75cef0f534, models::call_2bf97cffb54c404db531d664bc197d62, models::call_1d4523c7820144c2b2d2ca2288906842, models::call_547713695a844166803065afbd7898b5]

Configuration loading establishes the tensor collection, vocabulary and special-token IDs before architecture construction. EOS may be an integer or list; the list is retained while its first element becomes eos_token_id. Architecture configs map hidden_size, intermediate_size, layer count, query/KV head counts and RMS epsilon; missing KV-head count defaults to query-head count, and absent head_dim falls back to hidden_size // num_attention_heads. read_dict accepts integer-to-float conversion, so Gemma's integer query_pre_attn_scalar is accepted. Shared RoPE settings carry theta, scaling, partial-rotary factor and context limits with NEOX style. Phi3 alone overrides override_dynamic_seq_len to update the RoPE override before model construction. [source; models::call_3ac63e7f2f9b47a19663bc5481cec4f4, models::call_dd3f328daecd496386cfbd605b64d41b, models::call_2bf97cffb54c404db531d664bc197d62, models::call_9d20ee4c565847cabb6c03320692f7de, models::call_4887351d9a60417a885bfddf6d6a5753, models::call_c6430d9a6ea0416c9da7e20dc569d6b8]

The common model sequence is model.embed_tokens, one TransformerBlock per model.layers.i, model.norm, then lm_head. Llama-style blocks apply input_layernorm before attention and post_attention_layernorm before the gated MLP, with a residual addition after each branch. Attention maps q_proj/k_proj/v_proj/o_proj; the MLP maps gate_proj/up_proj/down_proj. With hidden width H, head width D, query heads Q, KV heads K and intermediate width I, checkpoint matrix shapes are [QD,H], [KD,H], [KD,H], [H,QD], [I,H], [I,H], [H,I]. This permits attention width QD to differ from residual width H, as Gemma requires. [source; models::call_2bf97cffb54c404db531d664bc197d62, models::call_c021c68c16074c79924f51bc562dbd04, models::call_dca98a08d5d946fc90014a026ac2cfee, models::call_e7afe3958bcb45a5b7b0a50fc903c63e, models::call_db291d1f84594376a6f5af18e14423c8]

TinyLlama supplies the straightforward Llama example: 22 layers, H=2048, I=5632, 32 query heads and four KV heads. Its BF16 header contains separate [2048,2048] Q/O matrices, [256,2048] K/V matrices, [5632,2048] gate/up matrices and [2048,5632] down matrices. Both embedding and untied output weights are [32000,2048]; layer norms and final norm have width 2048. The config selects SiLU, RMS epsilon 1e-5, theta 10000 and a 2048-position limit, matching the fields consumed by LlamaConfig. [source; models::call_3d439fb0c082421ba62000aa360d8048, models::call_1dd1bb7012a448d0bda2128a910a3c64, models::call_2bf97cffb54c404db531d664bc197d62]

Mistral reuses the same construction and naming. The pinned 7B checkpoint has 32 layers, H=4096, I=14336, 32 query heads and eight KV heads; its headers contain Q/O [4096,4096], K/V [1024,4096], gate/up [14336,4096], down [4096,14336], and separate [32000,4096] embedding/head weights. Sharding is not a layer boundary: layer 22's attention tensors are in shard one while its norms and MLP are in shard two. SafetensorsCollection indexes individual tensor names across all files, so constructors need no shard-specific logic. [source; models::call_1d4523c7820144c2b2d2ca2288906842, models::call_01763895acc1474281f6e06d62047559, models::call_520039ff856744319aa2926d9386ffd9, models::call_8c1d2ace022a449c8f2528f28a4e0f4b, models::call_554416c16d104a6ca72e3abb669fff86]

Qwen2.5-0.5B declares Qwen2ForCausalLM and fits the shared Llama layout with 24 layers, H=896, I=4864, 14 query heads and two KV heads. Its header additionally contains q_proj.bias [896] and k_proj.bias/v_proj.bias [128], but no O or MLP biases. Linear.load_fp16 retrieves each optional .bias rather than requiring a separate Qwen attention implementation. The config supplies theta 1000000, RMS epsilon 1e-6 and tied embeddings; the header contains model.embed_tokens.weight [151936,896] and no lm_head.weight. LlamaModel provides an embedding fallback for the output layer when tying is enabled. This is a loading fallback, not proof of shared runtime storage. [source; models::call_547713695a844166803065afbd7898b5, models::call_2be10d0a65f64354b457a45535cfa865, models::call_e094c1de1e71473cb3d47001b36d664b, models::call_2bf97cffb54c404db531d664bc197d62, models::call_bb207cd3479145ecbc9812227a226b51]

Phi3 retains separate logical Q/K/V and gate/up modules while accepting fused source tensors. Its pinned checkpoint has 32 layers, H=3072, I=8192 and 32 query/KV heads. Attention.__init__ assigns consecutive Q, K and V row ranges within self_attn.qkv_proj.weight [9216,3072]; GatedMLP.__init__ assigns gate then up ranges within mlp.gate_up_proj.weight [16384,3072]. Linear.load_fp16 first tries the separate logical weight, then slices the fused weight along its first axis and makes the slice contiguous. O remains [3072,3072], down [3072,8192], and embedding/head [32064,3072]. This adaptation preserves common execution and conversion interfaces rather than adding a fused-projection execution path. [source; models::call_9d20ee4c565847cabb6c03320692f7de, models::call_1e2940c5974e456483b2f31163df6f8b, models::call_0b1cc4fc0abf46c785334a9865bad356, models::call_7b6567aea2d74222bbed97a2a36f4788, models::call_c021c68c16074c79924f51bc562dbd04, models::call_dca98a08d5d946fc90014a026ac2cfee, models::call_bb207cd3479145ecbc9812227a226b51]

Gemma2 requires more than renamed tensors. The mirror has 26 layers, H=2304, I=9216, eight query heads, four KV heads and explicit D=256. Accordingly Q is [2048,2304], K/V [1024,2304] and O [2304,2048]; deriving D from H/Q would be wrong. Each layer has four norm vectors: input_layernorm, post_attention_layernorm, pre_feedforward_layernorm and post_feedforward_layernorm. Gemma2Model places post-norms before the branch residual additions, requests float outputs from those post-norms, and passes constant_bias=1 to every RMSNorm. RMSNorm's reference expression multiplies normalized activations by weight+1 rather than weight. Embedding normalization multiplies by sqrt(H), and the MLP selects the GELU multiplication kernel. ## Transformer composition, attention and rotary execution

### Problem

The initial implementation needs reusable transformer components that connect architecture-specific tensor names to attention, residual computation, quantized projections and KV-cache storage. [inference; modules::call_7da38eeaee9e439d95a96fcf2314bc71, modules::call_15326ae372f14506841a44dbf3171ec0, modules::call_fce3285be7144390b6bcf32cd83aad28]

### Reasoning

The design separates module composition from projection storage and attention backend selection. Architecture constructors assemble the blocks; Linear selects a weight implementation; Attention retains the same projection and rotary pipeline across cached and uncached execution. [source; modules::call_15326ae372f14506841a44dbf3171ec0, modules::call_c75755c132d5415ba1f1ba26d39baabe, modules::call_fce3285be7144390b6bcf32cd83aad28]

### Impact

The change introduces the module API and implementations for embeddings, linear projections, RMS normalization, attention, ordinary and gated MLPs, and transformer blocks. [source; modules::call_ac5f19fcfbbb419a815a01d90e8fdcc5]

### Explanation

Composition and device boundaries. Module is a custom abstract base, not a torch.nn.Module subclass. It registers children explicitly, recursively loads and unloads them, traverses itself before descendants, and aggregates quantization labels and weight counts. prepare_for_device moves an activation to the module's device. Model.forward runs top-level modules sequentially under inference_mode, moving the state at each boundary. The inspected Llama constructor demonstrates the interface: embedding → transformer blocks → final RMSNorm → vocabulary projection, with prepare_inputs calling prepare_for_attn. Architecture-specific assembly belongs to the models chapter. [source; modules::call_7da38eeaee9e439d95a96fcf2314bc71, modules::call_95ea20f830b944959bbee294c3f6f110, modules::call_15326ae372f14506841a44dbf3171ec0]

Embedding and residual precision. Embedding maps token IDs to hidden vectors, defaults its output to float32, marks itself as preferring CPU placement, and rejects a quantization map. Loading converts float32 weights to half, rather than universally converting every weight dtype. Its optional normalize operation multiplies embeddings by sqrt(hidden_size); it is not unit-length normalization. The shared to2 helper gives an explicit forward-call dtype precedence over the module default, otherwise preserving the tensor dtype. [source; modules::call_bb3a38ce9e3d4868a825142925c44187, modules::call_5b063db097b14b8da3a2475e47b31b55]

TransformerBlock.forward implements two sequential residual branches. Starting with x, it computes attention from attn_norm(x), optionally normalizes the attention output, and adds it to x in place. It then computes the MLP from mlp_norm(updated x), optionally normalizes that output, and adds it in place. Both input normalizations explicitly request half output, while the residual stream retains its existing storage dtype. The post-normalizations are optional; despite optional constructor annotations, the two input norms, attention and MLP are called unconditionally. [source; modules::call_da3a47a6b09e4f9f9a78198c04ce4b42]

Prefill is a targeted execution shortcut, not a different attention formula. Model.prefill marks only the last cache-affecting top-level module with params['prefill'] and stops after it. TransformerBlock checks that flag after attention has executed, returning the incoming residual without the attention addition or MLP. Earlier blocks still execute normally because their outputs feed later keys and values. The last attention call still includes its output projection. The shared params dictionary is mutable: prefill leaves its flag set, and Model.forward does not clear it. [source; modules::call_95ea20f830b944959bbee294c3f6f110, modules::call_da3a47a6b09e4f9f9a78198c04ce4b42, modules::call_fce3285be7144390b6bcf32cd83aad28]

Linear is the storage-independent projection interface. Loading tries EXL3 tensor groups across the primary and optional alternate keys before trying ordinary weights. Ordinary fused weights can be sliced by output-row ranges into separate logical projections; this does not fuse their forward calls. EXL3 loading passes trellis data, scaling/transform tensors and optional bias to LinearEXL3. forward delegates to the selected inner implementation and can then apply an in-place output softcap, c·tanh(x/c); zero disables this operation. The quantized-inference chapter covers the inner projection kernels. [source; modules::call_c75755c132d5415ba1f1ba26d39baabe, modules::call_bcedd362e436414f84fa4244a0ce27d0]

The conversion interface follows activation identity. Attention gives Q/K/V the same qmap suffix, and GatedMLP gives gate/up the same suffix, because each group consumes the same input. Linear.capture_H flattens leading dimensions into token rows, copies them to float32, accumulates XᵀX, and increments a row count. A first_key guard prevents another projection sharing that label from accumulating the same input again. convert_exl3 extracts weights and bias, drops the original inner layer, invokes quantize_exl3 with Hessian data, and installs a LinearEXL3 while preserving bias. This is the local handoff to the conversion and quantizer subsystems, not an accuracy measurement. [source; modules::call_7da38eeaee9e439d95a96fcf2314bc71, modules::call_c75755c132d5415ba1f1ba26d39baabe, modules::call_fce3285be7144390b6bcf32cd83aad28, modules::call_26adf7b7b18a4081ab25a66a8a8b5279]

Attention shapes. For input [B,T,H], projections produce Q of width Nq·D and K/V of width Nkv·D, then reshape to [B,T,Nq,D] and [B,T,Nkv,D]. RoPE modifies Q/K, not V. Attention output is flattened back to [B,T,Nq·D] before the output projection restores width H. A fused QKV checkpoint is interpreted as consecutive Q, K and V output-row ranges of those widths. Unequal head counts set the GQA flag; SDPA receives enable_gqa, while FlashAttention receives the differing head dimensions directly, with no explicit Python replication of K/V. [source; modules::call_fce3285be7144390b6bcf32cd83aad28]

Three attention routes share these projections. flash_attn_nc is the default; it rotates Q/K in place and calls flash_attn_func with causal masking enabled by default, the configured softmax scale, a symmetric window tuple, and attention-logit softcapping. flash_attn uses the corresponding KV-cache function. sdpa_nc instead transposes tensors to [B,heads,T,D], calls torch scaled_dot_product_attention, and transposes back. It rejects a nonnegative sliding_window and nonzero logit_softcapping, and omits sm_scale from its call. Both no-cache preparation functions reject a supplied cache. Here -1 is the default unrestricted-window sentinel, and 0.0 disables attention softcapping. [source; modules::call_fce3285be7144390b6bcf32cd83aad28]

Cache preparation turns a conventional batch into a page table. Given batch_shape, prepare_flash_attn checks batch capacity, requires exactly one of past_len or cache_seqlens, and requires cache sequence capacity to be divisible by PAGE_SIZE=256. It builds consecutive page indices reshaped to [B,capacity/256]. A scalar past_len is expanded to int32 sequence lengths and supplies the default rotary offset; per-sequence lengths supply default rotary positions unless positions or explicit position_ids are provided. An already-paged caller supplies block_table and cache_seqlens directly. These functions do not themselves schedule chunks or advance sequence lengths. [source; modules::call_fce3285be7144390b6bcf32cd83aad28, modules::call_f5f53c8bc09748278e29ff8f8b1140d8]

Cache ownership and attention handoff. Cache attaches layer objects to modules advertising kv_cache; Attention.load allocates those objects on its own device and unload frees them. The default FP16 layer stores each of K and V as [max_tokens/256,256,Nkv,D]. decode_flash_attn retrieves storage by layer_idx and passes it, freshly rotated K, V, block_table and cache_seqlens to flash_attn_with_kvcache. Thus cache mutation is delegated to the external attention call, while this wrapper performs no explicit length increment or writeback. Attachment traversal and attention layer indices must agree. The generation chapter owns the scheduling side of that interface. [source; modules::call_1031f039ce3040ec966cb984b8184492, modules::call_37590878a92642239eb439674e389b44, modules::call_fce3285be7144390b6bcf32cd83aad28]

Per-device metadata is cached inside params. Attention uses get_for_device for page tables, lengths and positional tensors. That helper memoizes each key/device conversion and explicitly assumes values remain read-only for the dictionary's lifetime. Consequently, replacing positional or cache-length metadata while retaining dev_cache can reuse stale device values; callers need a fresh dictionary or appropriate invalidation. [inference; modules::call_fce3285be7144390b6bcf32cd83aad28, modules::call_5b063db097b14b8da3a2475e47b31b55]

A bounded integer check verified the conventional-batch page layout: for B=2, capacity=512, page size 256, two KV heads and head dimension 8, all 16,384 element offsets from the generated page table matched the corresponding contiguous [B,capacity,Nkv,D] offsets. This verifies that indexing identity for the example, not FlashAttention's implementation or cache lifecycle. [execution; modules::call_891e21e9ed5441bda720b09ca96c950f]

RoPE construction. With rotary dimension d=int(head_dim·partial_rotary_factor), the default inverse frequency for pair i is theta^(−2i/d), with default theta=10000 and attention factor 1. NEOX pairs channels i and i+d/2. For angle position·frequency, the CUDA kernel computes (u·cos−v·sin, v·cos+u·sin), using float intermediates and rounding outputs to half. The attention factor multiplies both sine and cosine. A scalar starting position uses token_index+position; per-sequence positions use token_index+positions[batch]; explicit position_ids supplies each token position directly. The native wrapper prohibits supplying both positional tensors. [source; modules::call_84c531605ab2472fa94cc1f177b43724, modules::call_3b92d460ae3f477fba5fd5f7a2d35b5a, modules::call_50c85198ef6847d29a6f785ea15ee3d9]

RoPE scaling alternatives are implemented as frequency construction, not separate attention kernels. Linear scaling divides every frequency by its factor. Llama3 scaling compares wavelength 2π/frequency with old_context/high_freq_factor and old_context/low_freq_factor: short wavelengths retain their frequency, long wavelengths divide it by factor, and the middle band blends between them. Its fallback values are factor 8, low/high factors 1 and 4, and old context 8192. YaRN blends original and divided frequencies using a clamped ramp whose bounds come from rotation counts over the configured context; beta_fast=32 and beta_slow=1 are those default counts. Its default amplitude is 1+0.1·log(factor), and 0.001 prevents division by zero when ramp bounds coincide. LongRoPE/su selects per-frequency long or short factors according to the effective context override, but computes its amplitude sqrt(1+log(a/b)/log(b)) from configured maximum a and original maximum b when a>b. [source; modules::call_84c531605ab2472fa94cc1f177b43724]

Rotary implementation tradeoff. RoPE.apply makes inputs contiguous and calls a kernel that computes sine/cosine directly for each token; the kernel grid spans sequence and batch, and its head parallelism is bounded by 1024 threads per block. The alternative apply_torch maintains half-precision sine/cosine tables, expanded in 2048-position increments, and applies duplicated NEOX coefficients through _rotate_half. This trades cached coefficient storage against repeated trigonometric computation, but no timing comparison establishes which wins. The 2048 coefficient-growth interval is unrelated to the 256-token KV-cache page size. [inference; modules::call_84c531605ab2472fa94cc1f177b43724, modules::call_3b92d460ae3f477fba5fd5f7a2d35b5a, modules::call_f5f53c8bc09748278e29ff8f8b1140d8]

Scalar rotary checks covered 24 combinations of head dimensions 8/64/128, positions 0/1/255/8192 and amplitude factors 1/1.25. Rotating Q and K at the same position preserved their dot product multiplied by amplitude², with maximum double-precision error 7.11×10⁻¹⁵. Integer analysis also confirmed that the NEOX rotation/copy destinations cover a 128-channel head exactly once for rotary dimensions 128 and 64. In the partial case, however, 32 of the kernel's 64 unconditional frequency indices exceed the 32-element frequency array. [execution; modules::call_891e21e9ed5441bda720b09ca96c950f]

The partial-RoPE indexing result exposes a source-level safety concern rather than a demonstrated GPU failure: rope_kernel reads inv_freq[threadIdx.x] before testing whether that thread rotates a pair or merely copies unrotated channels. A partial frequency array can therefore be shorter than the launched frequency-index range. Separately, the Torch alternative applies full-vector _rotate_half and coefficient multiplication without explicitly separating an unrotated tail, so it should not be treated as a validated partial-RoPE fallback. [inference; modules::call_3b92d460ae3f477fba5fd5f7a2d35b5a, modules::call_84c531605ab2472fa94cc1f177b43724, modules::call_891e21e9ed5441bda720b09ca96c950f]

RMSNorm reduces over the last dimension without subtracting a mean: y_i=x_i·(w_i+constant_bias)/sqrt(mean(x²)+epsilon). Python flattens leading dimensions into rows and restores the original shape afterward. The native implementation uses float accumulation, fused multiply-add square sums and reciprocal square root; half inputs pass through its clamp helper. Four-element vector loads explain the required width divisibility by four, while 1024 threads are a launch choice rather than a normalization parameter. The Torch alternative casts normalized values back to the input dtype before weight multiplication, unlike the native float-intermediate path, so identical formulas do not imply bitwise-identical results. [source; modules::call_a67ebc4c0d62497497ec95ea00d7ef20, modules::call_ef3ecc67fa37464f8404b0b6a7e2e929]

MLP execution preserves leading batch/sequence dimensions while expanding hidden width H to intermediate width I and projecting back. MLP computes down(silu(up(x))). GatedMLP instead computes down(activation(gate(x))·up(x)), with SiLU or GELU selected at construction. A fused gate/up checkpoint is sliced into [0,I) and [I,2I), but gate and up still run separately. The activation/multiply kernel writes directly over the up result using half2 pairs. SiLU uses half exponential/reciprocal arithmetic; GELU uses the tanh approximation with sqrt(2/π)≈0.797884560803 and cubic coefficient 0.044715, then rounds to half before multiplication. [source; modules::call_26adf7b7b18a4081ab25a66a8a8b5279, modules::call_c75755c132d5415ba1f1ba26d39baabe, modules::call_1a8596f5c2f74ea48adc339bc546edf1]

### Uncertainty and limits

No model execution, CUDA kernel test, FlashAttention integration test or performance measurement was performed. The bounded Python checks validate scalar algebra and integer indexing, not FP16 rounding, CUDA fast trigonometry, reduction ordering or device memory safety.

RoPE support is incomplete: the CUDA GPTJ branch is a TODO, NONE launches no rotation kernel, and apply_torch also flags GPTJ as unfinished. Partial rotary dimensions expose an unconditional frequency-read bounds concern; its runtime consequences were not tested.

The SDPA route is not interchangeable with the FlashAttention routes for every configuration: it rejects sliding windows and attention softcapping, and does not pass the configured sm_scale. Q/K normalization remains a TODO in all three attention routes.

The native paths have narrower contracts than generic tensor APIs: RoPE requires FP16 Q/K; RMSNorm expects half weights and float/half activations; gated activation kernels operate on half2 pairs without validating dtype or handling an odd trailing element.

The inspected source supplies RoPE scaling formulas and defaults, but does not establish the empirical rationale or optimality of constants such as YaRN's 0.1 attention-factor coefficient and rotation thresholds.

## Dynamic generation, paged KV ownership and streaming contracts

### Problem

This root change introduces a generation layer that coordinates queued requests, bounded KV-cache capacity, prompt ingestion, sampling and streamed completion events. [source; generation::call_d9672fa51a404f818950034df076227f, generation::call_2721ad5dc7b74911b25c06844d765f3e]

### Reasoning

The central separation is between jobs owning logical sequences, a page table managing reusable physical cache slots, and model calls receiving explicit block tables and cache lengths. Reserving output capacity before starting a job simplifies subsequent decoding, but reduces concurrency when requested output budgets substantially exceed actual completions. [inference; generation::call_2407345feaf749438898ed7cbaad37b9, generation::call_98b25069b15546378633bd3a9addb24a, generation::call_2721ad5dc7b74911b25c06844d765f3e]

### Impact

The implementation supports dynamic admission, prefix-cache reuse, streaming and an asyncio wrapper. Optional probability/top-token reporting, banned-string restoration and multi-sequence handling have concrete suspected failure paths recorded in findings. [source; generation::call_b0a7466e39c2408baccea85cf352c66a, generation::call_5f647097e9fa4eb894e215f9065d472a, generation::call_98b25069b15546378633bd3a9addb24a, generation::call_d9672fa51a404f818950034df076227f]

### Explanation

Job accepts one CPU token tensor of shape (1, n), or a list of such sequences sampled collectively. It keeps original prompt IDs separately from mutable sequence IDs, per-job RNG state, output buffers and stop conditions. Token healing removes the final prompt token when every sequence has more than one token, restricts the first sample by its prefix and excludes the healed prefix from emitted text. max_new_tokens defaults through `max_new_tokens or 100`; stop tokens are populated only from supplied stop_conditions. [source; generation::call_e0b7a9d48e8b4a2d8af4f2563372f0e3, generation::call_5f647097e9fa4eb894e215f9065d472a]

enqueue prepares page requirements, assigns a serial and appends to pending_jobs. iterate first admits jobs, then performs one prefill round for each active job, then decodes ready jobs. Admission counts sequences rather than jobs against max_batch_size, and compares current page demand with unreferenced slots. Smaller jobs may bypass a non-fitting job, but its skip count increases for each admitted bypass; the default maximum of four eventually stops further bypasses. This fairness policy assumes each queued job can eventually fit, an assumption violated by the multi-sequence enqueue calculation in finding generation-capacity. [source; generation::call_d9672fa51a404f818950034df076227f, generation::call_2721ad5dc7b74911b25c06844d765f3e, generation::call_98b25069b15546378633bd3a9addb24a]

Cache creates one layer object per hidden layer and registers these objects with model modules advertising kv_cache capability. The default FP16 layer requires capacity divisible by PAGE_SIZE=256 and allocates separate zero-initialized K and V tensors shaped (capacity/256, 256, num_kv_heads, head_dim). free drops both tensors; detach_from_model removes registration. Partial-page copying copies only the requested token prefix in every layer. The generation path uses same-cache copies, so it does not depend on the cross-cache interpretation of Cache.copy_page's target argument. [source; generation::call_f2e94d17685c459e8942c7d0b9171aca, generation::call_5b2fb7d813604dcc901188dc2316dfd4, generation::call_166a40db4d66423d85716e2ddf799269, generation::call_98b25069b15546378633bd3a9addb24a]

Sequence.prepare hashes only complete context pages preceding the final input token: context_pages=(length-1)//256. It reserves ceil((length+max_new_tokens+healing_adjustment)/256) total pages, with the remainder private to that sequence. Context keys are 16-byte BLAKE2b digests incorporating the preceding page's digest, so identical local tokens under different prefixes are not intentionally shared. Mutable pages receive monotonically generated 16-byte placeholder keys, despite the random_hash name. [source; generation::call_2407345feaf749438898ed7cbaad37b9, generation::call_166a40db4d66423d85716e2ddf799269]

PageTable first shares a matching referenced context page, otherwise reclaims a matching unreferenced page, otherwise replaces the oldest unreferenced slot by access_serial. Reference counts move pages between referenced and unreferenced dictionaries. Reserving a private page backs up its old metadata; releasing an untouched reservation can restore that metadata because no KV data was overwritten. Actual writes disable restoration. Finished or cancelled active jobs release references without necessarily erasing cached contents. defrag is a no-op. [source; generation::call_2407345feaf749438898ed7cbaad37b9, generation::call_98b25069b15546378633bd3a9addb24a, generation::call_2721ad5dc7b74911b25c06844d765f3e]

Prefill leaves the last prompt token unevaluated for the first decode step. Its endpoint rounds kv_position+max_chunk_size down to a page boundary, then caps at sequence length minus one. The default 2048-token chunk spans eight pages; smaller chunks trade prompt throughput for shorter interruptions, but a positive chunk below 256 can make no progress. Existing complete pages advance the starting position. At a page boundary, prefill also searches pages with the same previous hash for a matching token prefix; a match longer than one token is copied in both main and draft caches when drafting is enabled. [source; generation::call_d9672fa51a404f818950034df076227f, generation::call_98b25069b15546378633bd3a9addb24a, generation::call_71675f5f32384b999bc3e24fb0965675]

Ready sequences satisfy kv_position == len(sequence_ids)-1. iterate_gen flattens them into a batch, builds an int32 physical block table and per-sequence cache lengths, and calls model.forward with attn_mode='flash_attn' and the cache. A logit mapping restores job boundaries afterward. get_input_ids_list records evaluated input IDs into private page metadata; receive_sample appends the sampled token and advances logical KV position. Completed pages gain chained hashes and can switch to an already referenced identical page, rebuilding the sequence's block table. [source; generation::call_d9672fa51a404f818950034df076227f, generation::call_2721ad5dc7b74911b25c06844d765f3e, generation::call_e0b7a9d48e8b4a2d8af4f2563372f0e3, generation::call_5f647097e9fa4eb894e215f9065d472a]

Draft decoding requires an equal-capacity draft cache. The draft model greedily generates num_draft_tokens, default four, then performs one extra prefill; the main model evaluates the current token plus proposals. Main-model samples are accepted until a proposal differs, after which remaining speculative page positions are rolled back and rejection counts increase. max_q_size defaults to eight and is used here to validate the requested draft count, not as a stored general generation limit. The draft batch construction counts jobs but writes one row per sequence, unlike ordinary decoding; finding generation-draft-rows records that mismatch. [source; generation::call_d9672fa51a404f818950034df076227f, generation::call_2721ad5dc7b74911b25c06844d765f3e]

The optional output contract is wired through several layers but is incomplete at sampling. Job stores return_probs and return_top_tokens; receive_logits passes logits, the first sequence's history, a 32-bit RNG value, tokenizer and token constraints to sampler.forward. CustomSampler returns only a token tensor, not probabilities or top-token arrays. receive_logits nevertheless promises the four-item tuple consumed by iterate_gen: token, top tokens, top probabilities and chosen probability. Its enabled optional branches are TODO/pass and leave local variables unbound. [source; generation::call_e0b7a9d48e8b4a2d8af4f2563372f0e3, generation::call_5f647097e9fa4eb894e215f9065d472a, generation::call_2721ad5dc7b74911b25c06844d765f3e, generation::call_a03cde1d538c4c7899988772344b6d5c, generation::call_cd9a8b253a4d41c3b244972d91fbdaf0]

Downstream buffers are already present: token IDs and chosen probabilities use (1,n), top-token IDs/probabilities use (1,n,k), and logits use (1,n,padded_vocab_size), with vocabulary width rounded up to a multiple of 32. receive_sample conditionally appends optional values; emit clones nonempty buffers into token_ids, token_probs, top_k_tokens, top_k_probs and logits, then clears their logical lengths. Unemitted terminal content is placed under held. Consequently enabling the missing sampling branches fails before these otherwise prepared output fields can be populated. return_logits separately records only the first logit row after the sampler call. [source; generation::call_d9672fa51a404f818950034df076227f, generation::call_5f647097e9fa4eb894e215f9065d472a, generation::call_98b25069b15546378633bd3a9addb24a, generation::call_b5a476dcfb0f457fb11266ea0f736158]

Streaming can emit metadata-only events while text is held. A stop token terminates without flushing held output. The token-budget test follows and flushes at new_tokens >= max_new_tokens-num_draft_tokens. Incomplete decoded characters are held while the replacement-character count is between one and four; a tokenizer re-decode may resolve them. Stop-string matching uses UTF-32 buffers: -2 means a possible trailing prefix, -1 no match, and a nonnegative result locates a full match. A full stop string truncates emitted text before the match, but token buffers remain token-granular rather than being split at that character boundary. [source; generation::call_5f647097e9fa4eb894e215f9065d472a, generation::call_98b25069b15546378633bd3a9addb24a, generation::call_71675f5f32384b999bc3e24fb0965675]

Banned strings are lowercased before matching. A possible prefix creates a checkpoint of held output before the current token, plus an offset and explored first-token IDs. A full match rewinds generated-token counts, sequence IDs and page validity, resets the checkpoint offset to zero and blocks explored first tokens on retry. This is logical invalidation, not restoration of saved KV tensors or RNG state. The held-token restoration uses the singular held_token attribute rather than held_tokens, allowing rejected IDs to escape in the emission; additionally the earlier token-budget branch can bypass banned-string checking entirely. [source; generation::call_e0b7a9d48e8b4a2d8af4f2563372f0e3, generation::call_5f647097e9fa4eb894e215f9065d472a, generation::call_98b25069b15546378633bd3a9addb24a]

AsyncGenerator is a cooperative wrapper, not a separate inference worker: its background task calls synchronous Generator.iterate, routes events into per-job asyncio queues, removes jobs on eos and yields with sleep(0). Exceptions are delivered to all currently registered jobs and end that iteration task. AsyncJob's iterator yields queued results until eos or a cancellation check. cancel releases/removes the underlying job, but does not wake an iterator already blocked in queue.get; close cancels the iteration task without explicitly clearing underlying generator jobs. [source; generation::call_b0a7466e39c2408baccea85cf352c66a, generation::call_d9672fa51a404f818950034df076227f]

The blocking generate helper tokenizes prompts, creates jobs, orders streamed text by serial and optionally returns terminal result dictionaries. Its abort event is checked after iterate returns, where it clears the queue, rather than being forwarded into the inspected prefill calls. Terminal streaming results include completion text, token counts, timing and cache-reuse counters; speculative counts are included when drafting. These counters describe recorded activity, not independently measured performance evidence. [source; generation::call_2721ad5dc7b74911b25c06844d765f3e, generation::call_5f647097e9fa4eb894e215f9065d472a, generation::call_98b25069b15546378633bd3a9addb24a]

The bounded transcription produced unbound-local errors for each enabled optional-output flag. It also showed a two-sequence request counted as one page at enqueue but two at admission, stale rejected token IDs after checkpoint restoration, and a zero prefill endpoint for chunk size 255. The asyncio model remained pending after its cancellation flag changed because no queue item woke the blocked consumer. [execution; generation::call_e36f63c9a36045e9a223f564c73c3bf6, generation::call_a4d625ee919d4f82aebf6fd49ffc7737]

## Composable sampling, Gumbel noise and native matching

### Problem

The initial implementation needs to turn model logits into token IDs while supporting greedy selection, stochastic sampling, distribution truncation and token restrictions. [inference; sampling::call_4538a78c98b145f4986006ae8cc7b68a, sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b, sampling::call_be4c5c0b1a314625aa01e9738baf1048]

### Reasoning

Sampling is organized as composable Python transformations, with native CUDA noise generation and separately exposed fused sampling primitives. This permits several sampling policies to share distribution handling rather than duplicating complete implementations. [inference; sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b, sampling::call_06541e8d34a44a78b9ca1cb120295f87]

### Impact

The public sampler package supplies default, greedy/argmax, categorical/Gumbel, top-k and top-p presets alongside custom sampling steps. [source; sampling::call_f2ecbd58ab334258a8527258c5e906d5, sampling::call_be4c5c0b1a314625aa01e9738baf1048]

### Explanation

CustomSampler.forward accepts logits, optional sequence IDs, a 32-bit seed, a tokenizer and blocked/allowed token lists. It flattens all leading logit dimensions into rows, runs the sampling steps and reshapes the selected IDs back to the original leading shape. sequence_ids is accepted but unused here. This is the local interface to generation; tokenizer.actual_vocab_size supplies the boundary for masking padded vocabulary entries. [source; sampling::call_4538a78c98b145f4986006ae8cc7b68a, sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b]

SamplingState distinguishes input logits, working logits, weights, normalized probabilities and sorted variants carrying token-index mappings. SS_Normalize uses softmax for logits and division by row sums for weights; SS_Sort sorts descending and records original token indices. SS_Sample maps a selected sorted position back through indices[row, position]. These distinctions allow truncation to discard probability mass without requiring immediate renormalization. [source; sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b]

Steps can substitute an alternative and request prerequisites: temperature 1 becomes SS_NoOp, top-k requests sorting, and top-p requests normalized sorted probabilities. However, CustomSampler.__init__ never advances its local state beyond INIT while constructing the list. Prerequisites are therefore chosen conservatively from INIT rather than from the preceding step's output. Runtime normalization and sorting often absorb the redundancy as no-ops; this is not a complete static state-transition planner. [source; sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b]

SS_Temperature divides logits by T, or raises probability weights to 1/T and marks them unnormalized. For positive T these describe the same relative distribution. TopPSampler can apply temperature before truncation, changing which candidates survive, or afterward, changing weights within the retained set. DefaultSampler chooses T=0.75 and top_p=0.75; these are preset choices, with no derivation supplied here. CategoricalSampler maps T=0 to greedy selection; TopKSampler also does so for k=1, and TopPSampler for p=0. [source; sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b, sampling::call_be4c5c0b1a314625aa01e9738baf1048]

Top-k keeps the first k sorted entries, replacing the rest with zero weights or negative-infinity logits; it uses a full sort rather than a partial top-k operation. Top-p computes inclusive cumulative probabilities and keeps entries whose cumulative sum is at most p, except that the first entry is always preserved. Thus it excludes the entry that first crosses the threshold, rather than retaining the smallest prefix reaching p. Top-k requires k>=1; SS_TopP requires 0<p<=1. [source; sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b]

A scalar translation confirmed this top-p boundary: weights [0.5, 0.3125, 0.1875] at p=0.75 become [0.5, 0, 0]. The second entry would cross to 0.8125 and is removed. The example uses exactly representable binary fractions, avoiding ambiguity from a cumulative-sum rounding boundary. [execution; sampling::call_6b5b9ed714b54dc7b3c5d05c99388907]

Token restrictions precede the sampling chain. Vocabulary padding is masked in the caller's logits in place; blocked/allowed filtering subsequently clones the tensor. Blocked IDs receive negative infinity, while an allowed list masks every ID outside that list. Applying both therefore retains allowed IDs minus blocked IDs. No local check prevents an empty candidate set. If no seed is supplied, Python chooses an integer in [0, 2^32-1]; an explicit seed also resets the global Python and Torch random generators. [source; sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b]

SS_Sample implements perturb-and-argmax: input logits use gumbel_noise_f16, working logits use gumbel_noise_f32, and probability weights use gumbel_noise_log, which takes their logarithm before adding noise. The latter two overwrite their working buffers. In ideal arithmetic, argmax(log(w_i)+G_i), with independent standard Gumbel noise, samples proportionally to positive weights w_i. Normalizing weights only adds a common offset to those scores, explaining why sampling can follow truncation without another normalization pass; zero weights become negative infinity. [inference; sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b, sampling::call_194a4230e264440da50efe62b5ee9ba6]

The CUDA Gumbel transform is -log(max(-log(max(u, 1e-20)), 1e-20)). The two 1e-20 guards prevent logarithms of zero at either endpoint; they are numerical guards, not filtering thresholds. Noise kernels initialize Philox using the supplied seed and flattened element index. FP32 kernels process one value per thread; FP16 processes half2 pairs, adds noise in float, then rounds back to half. Consequently, the unmodified-temperature FP16 route is not numerically identical to the FP32 working-logit route. [source; sampling::call_194a4230e264440da50efe62b5ee9ba6, sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b]

Binary64 evaluation of the guarded transform gives approximately -3.82976472 at u=0 and 46.05170186 at u=1. For logits [1,0,-1] and T=0.75, all 125 combinations of uniforms from {0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9} produced the same argmax using scaled logits, log normalized weights or log unnormalized weights. This checks the normalization identity on bounded inputs, not cuRAND output or native sampling frequencies. [execution; sampling::call_6b5b9ed714b54dc7b3c5d05c99388907]

Separate argmax_sample and gumbel_sample kernels fuse selection into one CUDA block per row, with 1024 threads scanning pairs of FP16 logits and reducing maxima. Their wrappers require two-dimensional half logits and long output tensors, and clamp max_logit to the stored width; zero means the full width. They run on the current CUDA stream. In contrast, CustomSampler materializes noisy values and delegates selection to torch.argmax. Fusion avoids that intermediate noise-output pass, but no speed advantage was measured. [source; sampling::call_3552dc40e6a64b049b6a42c3e9e9a42b, sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b]

The fused Gumbel kernel seeds Philox with threadIdx.x but not blockIdx.x, so rows reuse the same per-thread noise streams for a given seed. It also differs from the standalone noise kernels' per-element initialization. Its read2f boundary checks are reversed for an odd max_logit: the low lane tests idx<max_logit-1, while the high lane tests idx<max_logit. Neither this reader nor the FP16 noise kernel guards the second lane of an odd-sized half2 tail. [source; sampling::call_3552dc40e6a64b049b6a42c3e9e9a42b, sampling::call_194a4230e264440da50efe62b5ee9ba6]

Scalar evaluation of read2f's masks with six stored logits and max_logit=5 admitted indices [0,1,2,3,5], excluding valid index 4 and admitting padded index 5. This demonstrates the predicate issue without executing a native memory access. [execution; sampling::call_6b5b9ed714b54dc7b3c5d05c99388907]

Generic greedy composition has a separate defect: SS_Argmax's sorted branches assign the indexed result to state.state instead of state.sample, then overwrite state.state with DONE. They also omit the row-wise indexing used by SS_Sample. A chain reaching these branches cannot correctly return its sample. Plain ArgmaxSampler starts at INIT and does not exercise those branches. [source; sampling::call_8d8a770164d94ecebcb305734e6b053b, sampling::call_be4c5c0b1a314625aa01e9738baf1048]

partial_strings_match consumes byte buffers containing UTF-32 query text, packed patterns and byte offsets; dividing by four yields codepoint lengths and offsets. It scans patterns in list order, returning a full-match start index, -2 for a query suffix matching a pattern prefix, or -1 for no match. These are immediate returns, not a global earliest-match search. count_match_tensor compares raw 64-bit elements until the first mismatch, bounded by min(max_a, b.size(1)); it performs no local dtype, device or a-length validation. [source; sampling::call_3e95264c92d84568b2eff54ceea70db8]

A codepoint-level scalar translation showed the string-order consequence: query 'abc' with patterns ['abcd','bc'] returns -2, whereas reversing the patterns returns 1. Thus an earlier partial match can hide a later full match. The helper's result is suitable for a caller to distinguish incomplete suffixes from completed matches, but it does not itself implement buffering, banning or termination policy. ### Uncertainty and limits

The executed checks are standard-library scalar translations, not executions of PyTorch, CUDA kernels, cuRAND or native string functions. Floating-point calculations use Python binary64 rather than CUDA float intrinsics and FP16 rounding.

No sampling-frequency, performance or end-to-end generation measurements were made. The Gumbel arithmetic check establishes agreement on selected inputs, not distributional correctness.

CustomSampler does not validate positive temperatures or ensure that token restrictions leave a valid candidate. Its generic composition also contains a sorted-argmax assignment defect.

Native half2 paths lack odd-length tail handling. The fused sampling boundary predicate and shared per-row noise require caution; their effects were inspected and scalar-emulated, not tested on a GPU.

String helpers assume suitable buffers, nonempty patterns and host-readable tensor storage without enforcing those contracts locally. Generator-side handling of their return values was not inspected.

## Loading tensors and translating text

### Problem

The root change introduces the boundary between model artifacts and execution: named safetensors become device tensors, while tokenizer files and control-token configuration become token IDs, padded batches and decoded text. [source; loading-tokenization::call_20390b8ede7a43feb5fdae8e5e4eebd9, loading-tokenization::call_3b88e5159896417e992145b5a4d7bc3d]

### Reasoning

Both interfaces separate metadata interpretation from bulk processing. Python resolves tensor names, shapes and offsets before native reads; the tokenizer wrapper resolves control tokens and batching around a tokenizer.json-backed engine. This keeps model-specific metadata outside the native loader, but does not reproduce every Transformers tokenizer configuration behavior. [inference; loading-tokenization::call_20390b8ede7a43feb5fdae8e5e4eebd9, loading-tokenization::call_88bb22ff3573455583cabaafa18bf654, loading-tokenization::call_3b88e5159896417e992145b5a4d7bc3d]

### Impact

Callers gain selectable native/Python tensor loading, prefix-routed model variants, explicit BOS/EOS insertion and special-token rendering. The inspected implementation also leaves important compatibility boundaries around padding, EOS filtering, added-token handling and native I/O failure cleanup. [source; loading-tokenization::call_20390b8ede7a43feb5fdae8e5e4eebd9, loading-tokenization::call_88bb22ff3573455583cabaafa18bf654, loading-tokenization::call_3b88e5159896417e992145b5a4d7bc3d]

### Explanation

SafetensorsCollection scans *.safetensors directly rather than consulting an index JSON. read_header reads an eight-byte NumPy int64 header length, parses the following UTF-8 JSON and records the payload start. Metadata entries are excluded from the key-to-file map; later scanned files replace duplicate keys. get_tensor translates payload-relative data_offsets into absolute file offsets and asserts that shape product × element size equals the requested byte count. [source; loading-tokenization::call_20390b8ede7a43feb5fdae8e5e4eebd9]

The loader accepts I32, I16, F16, BF16 and F32. Missing required tensors raise an error; optional reads return None. BF16 is initially loaded as BF16, then converted to FP16 unless allow_bf16 is true. Prefix reads include an exact key or descendants separated by a dot. VariantSafetensorsCollection instead routes each read using the longest matching raw string prefix, allowing different tensor groups to come from different directories; its existence checks search all retained collections rather than validating that routing choice. [source; loading-tokenization::call_20390b8ede7a43feb5fdae8e5e4eebd9]

The default mt_fread path allocates a contiguous destination tensor and lazily caches five FILE handles per file. Pybind exposes stloader_open_file, stloader_read and stloader_close_file. Readers process 512 KiB blocks in stripes: worker i starts at i × block_size and advances by five blocks. Linux uses pread; the other branch uses a separate handle's _fseeki64/fread. Completed ranges enter a mutex-protected queue, and the GPU copy worker combines adjacent queued ranges before submitting cudaMemcpyAsync. [source; loading-tokenization::call_20390b8ede7a43feb5fdae8e5e4eebd9, loading-tokenization::call_88bb22ff3573455583cabaafa18bf654, loading-tokenization::call_ae55c5d675ed42a9af559dc163468a53, loading-tokenization::call_bb315f713e53464896a859a3d406e8b8]

Ownership differs by destination. CPU reads write directly into the tensor's storage. GPU reads allocate an ordinary malloc buffer for the entire tensor, populate it from disk, and copy completed chunks into the separately allocated GPU tensor on the captured current stream. The successful path joins workers, synchronizes the device and frees the staging buffer before returning. This is chunked scheduling, not bounded-size staging or direct disk-to-GPU I/O. Cached file handles remain open until close(), which resets them so later reads can reopen them. [source; loading-tokenization::call_88bb22ff3573455583cabaafa18bf654, loading-tokenization::call_20390b8ede7a43feb5fdae8e5e4eebd9]

The supported Python alternative seeks and reads into a bytearray, wraps it with torch.frombuffer, reshapes and calls .to(device); it does not use the imported mmap module. Compared with the native queue, this is a simpler whole-read path. Neither implementation establishes a speed advantage here. Loader metrics count source bytes and timed loading, report GiB/s using 1024³, and exclude the subsequent optional BF16-to-FP16 conversion. [source; loading-tokenization::call_20390b8ede7a43feb5fdae8e5e4eebd9]

A standard-library emulation checked nine sizes spanning empty input, block boundaries, the five-worker stride and a partial thirteenth block. Every size was partitioned into contiguous, nonoverlapping ranges with ceil(size / 524288) blocks and the expected worker assignment. The stride was 2621440 bytes. This verifies the small-case partition arithmetic, not concurrent I/O or transfer behavior; the source provides no measured rationale for choosing five readers or 512 KiB blocks. [execution; loading-tokenization::call_f06c193b5e1b4f86883235ef942c472a, loading-tokenization::call_ae55c5d675ed42a9af559dc163468a53, loading-tokenization::call_88bb22ff3573455583cabaafa18bf654]

The native failure path needs caution: the copy worker waits only for a nonempty queue, while a failed reader merely sets load_failed without notifying that condition variable. A read failure can therefore leave the copier asleep. load_failed is volatile rather than atomic, and TORCH_CHECK precedes staging-buffer cleanup. These are potential synchronization and resource-cleanup defects, not observed failures. The Python header parser also performs only limited validation, including no explicit file-bound or overlapping-range validation. [inference; loading-tokenization::call_88bb22ff3573455583cabaafa18bf654, loading-tokenization::call_20390b8ede7a43feb5fdae8e5e4eebd9]

Tokenizer always constructs the Hugging Face tokenizers engine from tokenizer.json. It additionally reads tokenizer_config.json and added_tokens.json, but not special_tokens_map.json or tokenizer.model. Config supplies BOS/EOS/PAD IDs from model config.json; a list-valued EOS retains its list in Config while its first entry becomes the scalar used by Tokenizer. Non-None model IDs take precedence over tokenizer-config strings or content objects. If those fields are absent, BOS/EOS default to IDs 1/2. Missing padding falls back to a truthy lookup of <pad>, then EOS when distinct from BOS, otherwise -1—not BOS as the nearby comment suggests. [source; loading-tokenization::call_3b88e5159896417e992145b5a4d7bc3d, loading-tokenization::call_b605efe605f94c61858b8ac445a508da]

Extended token mappings merge every added_tokens.json entry as special, followed by tokenizer_config added_tokens_decoder entries marked special. Non-special decoder entries are skipped; the initialized unspecial dictionaries are never populated here. encode_special uses escaped regex alternatives to insert mapped IDs between ordinary backend-encoded text segments. This manual path uses token content and IDs, not AddedToken lstrip/rstrip/normalized/single_word rules. With special handling disabled, encoding delegates to the backend with add_special_tokens=False; the wrapper itself does not force registered special spellings to become literal text. [source; loading-tokenization::call_3b88e5159896417e992145b5a4d7bc3d]

The pinned TinyLlama and Mistral configurations both map <unk>/<s>/</s> to 0/1/2. TinyLlama declares </s> as padding and requests right padding; Mistral declares no padding token and requests automatic BOS insertion. The wrapper instead always left-pads batches and defaults add_bos/add_eos to false. Thus these files supply token identities, not an automatically reproduced Transformers encoding policy. special_tokens_map.json corroborates the spellings but is not read by this implementation. [source; loading-tokenization::call_67e0041d2fd14d058cce35a6a2d24da1, loading-tokenization::call_44600a56a231473a9423b3f7b2d4dfda, loading-tokenization::call_c2f754dce73d4cda9456acad54b7bee9, loading-tokenization::call_634410ed286b48dfae41548888d8ba65, loading-tokenization::call_3b88e5159896417e992145b5a4d7bc3d]

Qwen2.5's pinned tokenizer configuration declares no BOS or unknown token, and uses <|endoftext|> at 151643 for EOS and padding. Special entries include <|im_start|>/<|im_end|> at 151644/151645, whereas tool-call and fill-in-middle entries are marked non-special and therefore skipped by the wrapper's added-token import. Phi-3 supplies an additional map covering 32000–32010, including <|endoftext|>, role markers and <|end|>; its EOS/padding is <|endoftext|>, while </s> at ID 2 is explicitly non-special. Phi's many rstrip=true entries highlight the difference between backend AddedToken behavior and the wrapper's literal regex split. [source; loading-tokenization::call_d56f4a87d068425b8e22e158b11f0bbc, loading-tokenization::call_52638cb20ad640a39d0c734bd9fbdda8, loading-tokenization::call_08561785b803475aa31ae33c291b04de, loading-tokenization::call_76a7be60da044a7cb79b4e581080bca9, loading-tokenization::call_3b88e5159896417e992145b5a4d7bc3d]

The Gemma mirror declares PAD/EOS/BOS/UNK as 0/1/2/3 and turn markers at 106/107. Its numerous non-special decoder entries, including whitespace and markup tokens, are not added to the wrapper's extended map and must be handled by tokenizer.json. The file requests left padding and automatic BOS insertion, but only left padding is inherent in this wrapper. Likewise, chat_template fields in TinyLlama, Qwen and Phi are stored in the loaded dictionary but never rendered by Tokenizer; callers must provide formatted text. [source; loading-tokenization::call_3c27009bec224393b56292aaf8b1f634, loading-tokenization::call_bd4b9851d3254e2c9f64b4c87f3c6d09, loading-tokenization::call_9320811537584c6eb3b54c9005efd81d, loading-tokenization::call_9fc0be27787443e7a683227f71bb0efd, loading-tokenization::call_44600a56a231473a9423b3f7b2d4dfda, loading-tokenization::call_d56f4a87d068425b8e22e158b11f0bbc, loading-tokenization::call_76a7be60da044a7cb79b4e581080bca9, loading-tokenization::call_3b88e5159896417e992145b5a4d7bc3d]

encode adds caller-requested BOS/EOS, left-pads to the longest sequence and optionally returns negative padding lengths. A scalar input produces shape (1, length). padding_mask compares IDs with pad_token_id, multiplies an integer mask by -65505 × 2, then casts to half. This targets a zero/unmasked versus negative-infinity/masked additive bias; sharing PAD with EOS also makes EOS positions match that mask. No tokenizer-config maximum length is enforced in encode. [source; loading-tokenization::call_3b88e5159896417e992145b5a4d7bc3d]

The bounded calculation reproduced left padding [[11], [12,13,14]] as [[0,0,11], [12,13,14]] with offsets [-2,0]. It also decoded binary16's maximum finite value as 65504 and calculated the mask's integer bias as -131010, confirming that the magnitude exceeds half's finite range. It did not execute PyTorch's conversion to infinity. [execution; loading-tokenization::call_f06c193b5e1b4f86883235ef942c472a]

decode_ without special rendering removes PAD, out-of-backend-vocabulary IDs and every EOS before its attempted EOS truncation, so the truncation branch cannot find EOS. Special rendering still removes PAD and IDs beyond the backend vocabulary, then renders mapped special pieces explicitly. Consequently, extended IDs can be represented in lookup tables yet be discarded by decode_. Also, decode returns a list for any rank-two tensor, including a one-row tensor, rather than following its documented batch-size-one string rule. [source; loading-tokenization::call_3b88e5159896417e992145b5a4d7bc3d]

A literal emulation of the ordinary decode filter transformed [11,2,12,0,99], with EOS=2, PAD=0 and vocabulary size 50, into [11,12]. The token after EOS survived. This checks the filtering order only; it neither invokes the backend decoder nor tests generation stopping. [execution; loading-tokenization::call_f06c193b5e1b4f86883235ef942c472a]

Tokenizer eagerly builds decoded-piece tables, a reverse piece map and an ordinal table. Piece enumeration probes ' t' to decide whether a space prefix is needed to preserve leading whitespace during single-token decoding; prefix queries then scan the piece table and cache up to 1000 results. These are local interfaces for token/text consumers, not a complete byte decoder: get_id_to_ord_list stores token_to_id results for backend-vocabulary entries, applying its byte-ordinal conversion only to appended entries. num_tokens also calls backend encode with defaults, unlike encode's explicit add_special_tokens=False calls. [source; loading-tokenization::call_3b88e5159896417e992145b5a4d7bc3d]

## Runtime initialization, native interfaces and storage utilities

### Problem

The runtime foundation connects the Python model and generation APIs to native tensor operations, extension compilation, device bookkeeping and reusable storage utilities. [inference; runtime::call_5868e458da17467b9c89a1d6ef7fe34f, runtime::call_5061701038f648dea98ebf72a83c4e31, runtime::call_7ecf7947cf1c47efb88ef22779246239]

### Explanation

The package initializer re-exports Config, Model, Tokenizer, cache classes, synchronous/asynchronous generation classes and samplers. The version is 0.0.1. This is a direct import-based public facade, not a lazy attribute loader; util similarly re-exports misc helpers. [source; runtime::call_5868e458da17467b9c89a1d6ef7fe34f, runtime::call_966f36d0b4e9417db0d5cfdbaee6064c, runtime::call_76cafc9bf36d40acb27b90efaf4effaa]

There are two extension-build paths. setup.py normally constructs a PyTorch CUDAExtension when Torch is installed; the presence of EXLLAMA_NOCOMPILE suppresses that step. At runtime, ext.py first imports exllamav3_ext and invokes torch.utils.cpp_extension.load only after ModuleNotFoundError. Both discover .c, .cpp and .cu files recursively. JIT compilation uses host -Ofast on non-Windows systems, versus setup.py's -O3; both use CUDA -O3 and -lineinfo. Windows JIT handling searches for MSVC when cl is absent and adds cuBLAS and virtual-environment library paths. Thus precompilation and deferred compilation are supported alternatives, but their host optimization settings are not identical. [source; runtime::call_5ca51a5440454da0a8aebddaf70ed7de, runtime::call_7ecf7947cf1c47efb88ef22779246239]

maybe_set_arch_list_env preserves an explicit TORCH_CUDA_ARCH_LIST. Otherwise, on CUDA builds it collects visible-device capabilities, clamps each to the highest SM capability advertised by Torch, deduplicates and sorts the targets, and appends +PTX to the last target. It runs when arch_list is imported and again before JIT loading. The source explicitly assumes the user's NVCC matches Torch's build toolchain; this clamp is a compatibility heuristic, not independent compiler detection. [source; runtime::call_1f6a408c9f964ff0b24d8f0fc2c8f7b9, runtime::call_7ecf7947cf1c47efb88ef22779246239]

bindings.cpp exposes ordinary pybind functions for safetensors I/O, normalization, softcapping, Hadamard construction/transforms, trellis quantization and packing, reconstruction, EXL3 GEMM, FP16 GEMM, rotary embeddings, gated activations, sampling and string matching. This is the local interface to the loading, quantizer, quantized-inference, modules and sampling subsystems. The inspected activation, normalization, softcap and hgemm wrappers select the input tensor's device with OptionalCUDAGuard and use Torch's current CUDA stream; their outputs are supplied by the caller rather than allocated by these wrappers. [source; runtime::call_5061701038f648dea98ebf72a83c4e31, runtime::call_05a03abedb9e4a0790e7191a29d22045, runtime::call_1f5dec05d9a8471a8faaa72c7c695c79, runtime::call_0c674e87bc0648f29ec5b93ce185f80b, runtime::call_e4837517c0694e618ab22484f0ccc242]

DeviceContext is lightweight bookkeeping: a global dictionary keys contexts by configuration UUID and device string. Acquisition creates or reuses a context and increments its reference count; release decrements it and removes the entry at zero. The context stores the configuration and device, but does not itself allocate GPU buffers or implement a cache scheduler. [source; runtime::call_a88226c9aeba42faa5f91e05557beaa9]

Memory budgeting adjusts Torch's per-process allocator fraction rather than allocating a reservation buffer. After a small 32×32 device computation, set_memory_fraction_reserve uses (free − reserve)/total; set_memory_fraction_use uses min((already_allocated + use)/total, 1). The reserve path does not clamp invalid budgets. unset_memory_fraction restores 1.0, while free_mem combines Python garbage collection with empty_cache. list_gpu_tensors is a diagnostic traversal of globals, thread frames and reachable containers, reporting tensor-object sizes in integer MiB; it is not a storage-deduplicated allocator accounting system. [source; runtime::call_ee048f7e478044fcaf047cba0fc3bea0]

SeqTensor separates logical sequence length from allocated capacity. Its own PAGE_SIZE is 256, matching the separately declared shared constant numerically. Appending grows storage with torch.cat when end_pos >= capacity, selecting (end_pos // 256 + 1) × 256, then copies into a narrow view. Consequently an exact page boundary still gets another page. clear and truncate retain storage; torch and torch_slice return views, whereas slice and clone construct copied SeqTensors. get_for_device separately caches device copies inside a dictionary and explicitly requires tensors to remain read-only; buffered_arange extends that caching to reusable ranges. [source; runtime::call_13cd868864824b78a2c5a846ec16b211, runtime::call_43be88fe24cb4a2e8164c36d29ec747b]

A standard-library simulation of 4,096 single-element appends preserved a positive spare capacity of at most 256 elements, performed 16 growth operations and ended at capacity 4,352. This verifies the capacity arithmetic, not tensor copying. Fixed-size growth bounds unused sequence slots, but unlike geometric growth it repeatedly copies the accumulated allocation as a sequence becomes longer. [execution; runtime::call_2e71821ca4054a44b3cfc56737cee611, runtime::call_13cd868864824b78a2c5a846ec16b211]

hgemm implements row-major C = A @ B through column-major cuBLAS by reversing the operands and calling with dimensions (N, M, K), leading dimensions (N, K, N), alpha = 1 and beta = 0. This represents Cᵀ = BᵀAᵀ without an explicit transpose buffer. It validates half dtype, rank two and compatible dimensions, but does not check contiguity or matching devices. [source; runtime::call_e4837517c0694e618ab22484f0ccc242]

Scalar address emulation matched ordinary row-major multiplication for all 512 combinations of M, K and N from 1 through 8. This checks the hgemm operand reversal and leading-dimension identity, not cuBLAS numerical accuracy or speed. [execution; runtime::call_2e71821ca4054a44b3cfc56737cee611]

The shared elementwise kernels emphasize vectorized caller-owned buffers. silu_mul and gelu_mul process two half elements per thread with 256-thread blocks and fuse activation with multiplication by the second input. SiLU uses half arithmetic; GELU converts to float for 0.5x[1+tanh(c(x+0.044715x³))], with c documented as sqrt(2/π). tanh_opt uses an exponential identity under USE_ROCM or older CUDA architecture/runtime conditions, otherwise tanh.approx.f32. softcap computes scale × tanh(x/scale), using scalar float or paired half loads with float intermediates and 1,024-thread blocks. [source; runtime::call_05a03abedb9e4a0790e7191a29d22045, runtime::call_1a2e972b238a4e02be31687c7b982ad1, runtime::call_0c674e87bc0648f29ec5b93ce185f80b]

The bounded computation gives sqrt(2/π) = 0.7978845608028654, consistent with GELU's literal. It also exposes vector-tail constraints: activation indexing leaves the last element unwritten for tested odd lengths 1, 3 and 513. Emulating half-softcap launch/index arithmetic gives an access beyond length 3, while lengths 1 and 2,049 leave their last element unprocessed. These are indexing results, not observed GPU memory faults. [execution; runtime::call_2e71821ca4054a44b3cfc56737cee611, runtime::call_05a03abedb9e4a0790e7191a29d22045, runtime::call_0c674e87bc0648f29ec5b93ce185f80b]

rms_norm launches one 1,024-thread block per row, loads groups of four elements, accumulates squares in float and reduces first across lanes and then across warps. It computes x × (w + constant_bias) / sqrt(mean(x²) + epsilon), supporting half/float input-output combinations with half weights. Width must be divisible by four. Half inputs are clamped to ±65,504 before normalization; float inputs are not. The wrapper checks weights and shapes, but classifies every non-float input/output as half, so its final invalid-dtype branch does not protect against arbitrary other dtypes. [source; runtime::call_1f5dec05d9a8471a8faaa72c7c695c79, runtime::call_79cc01bdbd9e4adc8148a73f8bb31e00]

The clamp value 65,504 is exactly the largest finite binary16 number: (2 − 2⁻¹⁰) × 2¹⁵. A standard-library binary16 decode of bit pattern 0x7bff confirmed that value. This explains the numeric boundary without establishing how the GPU clamp handles every exceptional input. [execution; runtime::call_2e71821ca4054a44b3cfc56737cee611, runtime::call_79cc01bdbd9e4adc8148a73f8bb31e00]

The low-level utility layer supplies aligned half4/half8 aggregates, 64/128-bit vector transfers, bit reinterpretation/XOR helpers and shape/dtype assertion macros. static_for_pack turns a list of integer constants into compile-time callback instances for specialized kernel dispatch. reduction.cuh supplies 32-lane shuffle reductions and shared-memory aggregation for argmax, half maximum and float sum. Unlike the sum/max helpers, block_reduce_argmax reads all 32 shared warp slots without a num_threads guard, so its use requires all those slots to have been populated. [source; runtime::call_79cc01bdbd9e4adc8148a73f8bb31e00, runtime::call_d1987f5839214d918f7762652785b145, runtime::call_503d104d964246f0a1ef0083d4e58b56]

ptx.cuh exposes m16n8k16 tensor-core operations with either float or half accumulators, shared-memory matrix-fragment loads, 16-byte asynchronous global-to-shared copies, commit/wait groups and GPU-scope barrier operations. These form the local native machinery relevant to quantized inference: data can move into shared memory and then tensor-core fragments without an ordinary scalar matrix representation. Additional helpers provide low/high unsigned multiplication and extraction from a combined 64-bit word. The source warns against its older m8n8k4 helper and records a suspected Blackwell issue with explicitly predicated asynchronous copies; those comments are not measurements reproduced here. [source; runtime::call_53bdd4630bb64252aae60fdfe7359d81]

The remaining Python utilities support configuration and diagnostics. read_dict tries ordered alternative keys, including nested '->' paths, checks types and permits integer/float conversions when appropriate; None is treated as missing. disk_lru_cache persists JSON-keyed results through shelve beside the launching script, but implements no eviction despite its name. synchronized uses one shared reentrant lock. Timer measures wall-clock intervals without GPU synchronization; cuda_sync_active synchronizes only devices reporting allocated Torch memory. ProgressBar wraps Rich, becomes inactive for empty text and marks non-transient progress complete on context exit. [source; runtime::call_0b9030c8ef084af5bc530b650f5f6d52, runtime::call_6fe1cdea0ba343b1bc0aec90d3bb50f4, runtime::call_599c243890574eb2973685b9ab5982a3]

### Problem

The early preview needs installation instructions, runnable usage examples, and ways to inspect numerical correctness and model quality; the README explicitly invites testing rather than presenting a production-ready replacement for ExLlamaV2. [source; usage-evaluation::call_5ba43b65301a41e3b392317aa6259629]

### Explanation

Installation offers two paths: install requirements.txt followed by pip install ., or set EXLLAMA_NOCOMPILE for a JIT-mode installation. setup.py uses the presence of that environment variable—not its value—to disable precompilation. Otherwise, when Torch is already available, it gathers C/C++/CUDA sources into a CUDAExtension built through PyTorch. The README also permits running scripts directly from the checkout. It requires FlashAttention-2, reports no release builds or ROCm support, and identifies cache quantization and tensor parallelism as missing; a HIP compiler flag in setup.py does not establish working ROCm support. [source; usage-evaluation::call_5ba43b65301a41e3b392317aa6259629, usage-evaluation::call_eab125d8f4e6444f961f305b67a1a3bf]

Dependency installation is not interchangeable across the two lists: requirements.txt specifies Torch >=2.6.0, flash_attn >=2.7.4.post1, tokenizers >=0.21.1, and NumPy >=2.1.0, while setup.py permits Torch >=2.2.0 and omits flash_attn and tokenizers from install_requires. Evaluation dependencies are separated into requirements_eval.txt, including HumanEval 1.0.3 and several comparison backends; requirements_examples.txt adds blessed for the terminal display. MANIFEST.in explicitly includes Hadamard data files. [source; usage-evaluation::call_29178b6ca290458d8a8f78edc16b2604, usage-evaluation::call_eab125d8f4e6444f961f305b67a1a3bf, usage-evaluation::call_a7ba86a0a81d4f8283f00898026f4564, usage-evaluation::call_257188d350ae46ab8c823584dac0347f, usage-evaluation::call_bbda9840f39443a696c08ad4f99e191a]

The documented conversion interface is python convert.py -i <input_dir> -o <output_dir> -w <working_dir> -b <bitrate>. The working directory holds checkpoints and quantized tensors pending compilation and should fit an output-model copy. Resumption requires an explicit -r/--resume, unlike the documented EXL2 default. These instructions connect users to conversion; they do not demonstrate conversion speed or successful recovery. [source; usage-evaluation::call_5ba43b65301a41e3b392317aa6259629]

examples/generation_loop.py exposes the low-level inference contract: construct Config, Model, and Cache before model.load(); tokenize the prompt; prefill all but its final token; then forward that final token with past_len equal to context length minus one. Each sampled token is appended and becomes the next input. The example supplies flash_attn, cache, and a rectangular batch_shape of (1, 2048), checks token stop conditions, and streams vocabulary pieces. examples/generator.py offers the higher-level alternative: generate() for complete single/batched outputs, or enqueue Job objects and route iterate() result chunks by identifier. Its temperature sweep demonstrates a sampler option, not a quality evaluation. [source; usage-evaluation::call_c3116b3fe85645bdb4972833dec8beee, usage-evaluation::call_286847ff6d4843ee92854aadfd7f95ac]

The interactive entrypoint is python examples/chat.py -m <model_dir> -mode <mode>. It formats and retokenizes conversation history, reserves room for the response, removes oldest turns when necessary, and streams a Job's text updates. chat_templates.py supplies raw, llama3, chatml, phi, mistral, and gemma formats with explicit BOS and stop-token policies; these are application-side templates, not architecture validation. The separate common.py helper labels its templates approximate, contains an extra literal 'n' in the Mistral prompt, and has no Mistral branch in get_stop_conditions. [source; usage-evaluation::call_5ba43b65301a41e3b392317aa6259629, usage-evaluation::call_6e4782a2547d422a9ed04355d8e03698, usage-evaluation::call_c3954153f80047d0a343ef129e4c1cec, usage-evaluation::call_c72d5456515447baace33fcf5addea2a]

dynamic_gen.py demonstrates queueing many prompts against a shared 32768-token cache, a batch limit of 16, and 2048-token prefill chunks. Its comment states the tradeoff: smaller chunks reduce pauses when jobs start but sacrifice prompt-ingestion throughput. async_generator.py instead wraps jobs as async iterators gathered concurrently, explicitly cancels one early-exited job, and closes the generator. banned_strings.py contrasts jobs with and without phrase suppression and displays suppressed_text separately; it also changes min_new_tokens between those conditions, so it is a feature demonstration rather than a controlled comparison. [source; usage-evaluation::call_b26d0c0066c545de9a3937345fcc8a8b, usage-evaluation::call_a17f1195bae7453d93d26775c14a1880, usage-evaluation::call_dd22cd22e9fd4d388f594dc1a262ea84]

eval/ppl.py measures next-token likelihood on Wikitext-2 raw test text joined with double newlines. It tokenizes with the loaded model's tokenizer, makes 2048-token windows at stride 512, defaults to 100 rows, and forwards each window without a KV cache using flash_attn_nc. It slices logits to the actual vocabulary, applies float32 log_softmax, gathers the following token's log probability, and reports exp(-sum(log probability)/target count). Every window contributes all 2047 next-token targets; overlapping targets are not masked away. [source; usage-evaluation::call_18a766598ce94204a10329e055e71cb6]

eval/compare_q.py separates dataset preparation from backend loading and forwarding through named function tables. Dataset and perplexity functions use disk-cache decorators; --clear_cache invalidates selected test_ppl entries. Model specifications are JSON arrays, despite the CLI help saying JSONL, and wildcard filenames can combine them. The runner emits JSON results and optionally plots perplexity against decoder bits per weight or decoder-plus-head storage. Unlike ppl.py, it does not slice the logits vocabulary before softmax. The supplied Wikitext specifications use length 2048 and stride 512, capped at 20 or 100 windows. [source; usage-evaluation::call_2daf930a08484ddd86794564f60f9d70, usage-evaluation::call_3d36342c31f649b6bd61a360d92a9fe4, usage-evaluation::call_618c2d9afbf840db981f5b9c4fbe8431, usage-evaluation::call_bb74ed980c314a41ac907b2269ae4d6a]

A bounded Python check reproduced the window-target indexing. Assuming enough source tokens, 20 windows score 40,940 target occurrences but only 11,775 distinct target positions; 100 windows score 204,700 occurrences over 52,735 distinct positions. Maximum multiplicity is four. Thus the configured statistic weights overlapping predictions, including predictions with different available context, rather than scoring each corpus position once. This checks indexing only, not tokenization or perplexity values. [execution; usage-evaluation::call_6993685a93164d89bf14c7050229fd61]

Backend adapters normalize their outputs to logits but retain different execution paths. ExLlamaV2 uses autosplit with a cache required for loading but not used by its evaluation forward. ExLlamaV3 loads for 2048-token output and forwards in flash_attn_nc mode; it can also overlay tensor files supplied alongside a model directory. llama.cpp resets state for each sequence, requests all logits with a 2048 context, and transfers scores to CUDA. Transformers loads in half precision on cuda:0 or device_map='auto' and receives the common token IDs. Sharing IDs controls one input variable but does not itself establish vocabulary compatibility across artifacts. [source; usage-evaluation::call_2a11de57e9d7487f88ca83a38aaa3389, usage-evaluation::call_3da8b9e429ec4bf6bc0367db53118a02, usage-evaluation::call_5c3cf9f4c40e455e94a35b68c8771d57, usage-evaluation::call_322572cabc7f499da0eb4ea38eee3fa7]

The comparison plot's 'VRAM' is calculated tensor storage, not measured peak GPU allocation. Adapters count decoder linear weights/quantization tensors and the output head, then convert bits to bytes and divide by 1024^3. This excludes runtime caches and workspaces. Accounting is backend-specific: GGUF recognizes projection names and falls back to token embeddings for a tied head; Transformers recognizes several quantized module classes and uses out_features >= 0.9*vocab_size as a head-identification heuristic. That 90% cutoff is an implementation heuristic, not a verified architecture invariant. [source; usage-evaluation::call_2daf930a08484ddd86794564f60f9d70, usage-evaluation::call_2a11de57e9d7487f88ca83a38aaa3389, usage-evaluation::call_3da8b9e429ec4bf6bc0367db53118a02, usage-evaluation::call_5c3cf9f4c40e455e94a35b68c8771d57, usage-evaluation::call_322572cabc7f499da0eb4ea38eee3fa7]

The model specifications configure comparisons for Llama-3.1 70B/8B Instruct, Llama-3.2 1B Instruct, and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3. They include EXL2/EXL3 bitrate sweeps and alternatives such as GGUF, AQLM, AWQ, VPTQ, BNB, and an HF FP16 baseline. These files contain local paths, dispatch names, and labels—not scores. Even labels need care: the 70B EXL3 specification assigns the same 'EXL3 1.6bpw H3' label to two different paths. [source; usage-evaluation::call_061ab7db673c47b88599a3920f90a44c, usage-evaluation::call_ced792c6e0b6411382ad1f1e74553538, usage-evaluation::call_f7a0d5772c1242a38500fefd099fd517, usage-evaluation::call_f62eaa3ce53440aeb28af1dd87f9e249, usage-evaluation::call_17573d466fde476eb9705f757618c598, usage-evaluation::call_0e984eaddae548b2968dfa6ddb4696cf, usage-evaluation::call_4cbeb21284504b02bdf66aee33e764c9, usage-evaluation::call_6b2586982ee241a49b64a705af7c0524, usage-evaluation::call_916436e158a7423fb3d046fbc7898df9, usage-evaluation::call_59194335c63a49708737968a58d32890, usage-evaluation::call_1da4c7b906244756876109d6d4e03c06]

eval/model_diff.py is a layerwise divergence diagnostic, not a language-quality score. It tokenizes Wikitext with model A's tokenizer, advances independent hidden states through zipped top-level module lists, and loads/unloads one module at a time to limit residency. It prints mean per-row Frobenius-relative error and maximum absolute difference divided by model B's row norm. Differences therefore accumulate through earlier modules; the script does not isolate each layer using identical incoming activations or assert that the two module lists match. [source; usage-evaluation::call_bc183882bd294050b710f3773cb3e4d6]

eval/humaneval.py generates code-completion samples and optionally delegates functional checking to evaluate_functional_correctness. Defaults are 200 samples per task, top_p=0.6, temperature=0.6, and a 768-token cap; jobs use token healing, a six-token minimum, EOS stopping, and a generator batch limit of 256. The collector additionally ends a sample at a non-indented final line, cancels unfinished jobs, and writes task_id/completion records to JSONL. Prompt formatting and this extraction heuristic are part of the evaluation protocol. Merely generating the file does not establish functional correctness or a pass rate. [source; usage-evaluation::call_6d844bdcbb4445019d0e34c5689a03a2]

The science scripts probe narrower mechanisms. codebook_eval.py decodes all 65,536 16-bit states, prints RMS, plots a 256-bin histogram, and draws 20,000 transition pairs for each K=1..8 using ((state<<K)|new_bits)&0xFFFF. gumbel_eval.py compares GumbelSampler with softmax-plus-multinomial on one synthetic 128,256-logit distribution, using 250,000 samples and diagnostics every 10,000. Its 1e-5 floor clamps both empirical and reference probabilities; despite comments about ignoring small probabilities, those entries are not removed. [source; usage-evaluation::call_bb6c5266aa4e40fa94e40eaadb5dfce4, usage-evaluation::call_e118ac7813bd4326a5d1089682613765]

science/qgemm_benchmark.py times kernel variants rather than checking correctness. It sets K=8, tries several matrix and batch shapes on three hardcoded devices, queries the preferred kernel, skips incompatible variants, performs ten warmups, and averages 200 calls. It cycles cloned buffers whose nominal combined size exceeds an assumed 512 MiB cache budget to reduce L2-residency bias; that number is an assumption, not detected hardware capacity. Scalar .item() reads bracket timing to wait for GPU results. The table marks the preferred kernel 'OK' when its relative latency gap from the fastest tested variant is at most 1%; no resulting timings are supplied here. [source; usage-evaluation::call_bff2d2ca24ad41f7b7c5eb406d361bb9]

The numerical tests compare specific paths rather than proving end-to-end correctness. test_ext_norm.py checks RMS normalization against float32 reference arithmetic across input/output dtypes and shapes, including in-place operation, with rtol=atol=1e-3; it separately exposes synchronized benchmark cases. test_rope.py compares native and Torch application for scalar offsets, batched offsets, and explicit position IDs at 1e-6 tolerance, but enables only NEOX style with theta=1, full rotation, and no scaling. test_qgemm.py compares direct quantized forwarding with reconstructed forwarding for eight selected projections/head and ten batch sizes, accepting rtol=atol=0.05. Both GEMM paths use the same quantized model, so this is not a comparison to original unquantized weights. [source; usage-evaluation::call_5d03729f7e7b42d5829a2702b8d5e715, usage-evaluation::call_35a486c39bf44f24b002f98fec246302, usage-evaluation::call_57d8965abcfb44639e0b6594116633fb]

test_quant_fn.py checks three distinct properties: tail-biting and normalized MSE for random tiles (currently parametrized only at K=1), near-lossless re-encoding of synthetically valid decoded sequences for K=1..8, and layer conversion/reconstruction using Hessians captured from random activations. Conversion checks use explicit K-dependent proxy-error and weight-tolerance tables. assert_close_mr permits a specified fraction of elements outside torch.isclose: 1% for original-versus-quantized weights and 0.1% for quantizer output versus reconstruction. The latter uses 1e-3 relative/absolute tolerances, with a source comment attributing necessary tolerance to float32 quantization versus float16 reversal of regularization. These tests do not evaluate representative calibration data or full-model quality. [source; usage-evaluation::call_65ca26b5fbc14ea39e50a5b35f6d2646, usage-evaluation::call_6b0bbddd462c44feb90157cd3ad3f343]

test_sampler.py constructs reference top-k and top-p distributions from synthetic logits and compares sampled frequencies using sum((observed-expected)^2/expected), after flooring probabilities at 1e-5. Its top-p reference retains entries whose cumulative probability is <=p and always retains the first entry. Thresholds are 0.01 for top-k and 0.02 for top-p, with sample-count caps of 10,000 and 20,000. This probability discrepancy, also used by gumbel_eval.py, is not directly a count-based Pearson chi-square test with a reported significance level. [source; usage-evaluation::call_c5a0422ba38d4db8ad44e042058123d8, usage-evaluation::call_e118ac7813bd4326a5d1089682613765]

A scalar illustrative check with reference probabilities [0.7, 0.2, 0.1] and observed frequencies [0.68, 0.22, 0.1] produced probability discrepancy 0.00257143. For 10,000 samples the corresponding count-based Pearson statistic was 25.7143, a factor of 10,000 larger. This confirms the normalization distinction without testing any sampler. The computation uses Python double precision; the inspected GPU diagnostics use float tensors, and flooring can further change the statistic. [execution; usage-evaluation::call_6993685a93164d89bf14c7050229fd61]

generator_stresstest.py repeatedly changes queue depth and checks whether generated number lists remain consecutive, printing cached-page/token counts and 'OK!' or 'Sus!'. It exercises model generation under cache reuse, but failures are printed rather than asserted and model counting errors can confound cache diagnosis. There is also a source-visible empty-queue edge: a selected target depth of zero can leave the final randint call with bounds (0, -1). Thus the script is a useful manual stress probe, not an established exhaustive paging test. [inference; usage-evaluation::call_c18497bcc93b4b4f89a821b3b7f16483]

### Uncertainty and limits

No installation, model inference, CUDA test, HumanEval evaluation, or performance benchmark was run. The successful execution checks only window-index arithmetic and an illustrative statistical formula using standard-library Python.

Example and evaluation paths refer to local model artifacts without pinned weight revisions. Several scripts hardcode cuda:1, cuda:2, or cuda:3; reproducing them requires editing paths and device selection.

The inspected dependency lists do not establish a reproducible environment: setup.py and requirements.txt differ, llama_cpp is imported but absent from requirements_eval.txt, and pytest plus its benchmark fixture are used without being listed in the inspected requirements.

Numerical tolerances and statistical cutoffs are authored acceptance criteria, not established error guarantees. Their empirical justification is not supplied by these files.

Cross-backend tokenizer equivalence, total runtime VRAM, model-quality rankings, and speedups remain unverified. Included model specifications and README performance assertions are not new measurements.
